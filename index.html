Good morning, it's Monday, November 23, 2026.

The story dominating both the business and technology pages is the artificial-intelligence trade, and the scandal at its center. The Times reports that Alice Huang, the woman at the heart of the Nvidia affair, stayed in touch for more than a year with an official of China's top spy agency while carrying on an affair with Jensen Huang, who was then Nvidia's chief executive, and that U.S. intelligence tracked those contacts. Huang resigned on November 12 after admitting to one affair and facing allegations of another with a subordinate. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, is interim CEO, and the board chair said last week the company does not intend to bring him back. Reuters reports that U.S. agencies are still trying to determine what Alice Huang may have passed to Beijing; China denies any espionage operation and Huang denies compromising national security. The Times says her last known move was opening a company in Hong Kong this summer and that she is probably in China, beyond the reach of U.S. authorities. The Journal adds a money angle: Jensen and Lori Huang control about 870 million Nvidia shares, worth more than $109 billion. Nvidia itself keeps delivering, with quarterly revenue of about $115.8 billion, more than double a year ago, though the shares remain down for the year.

That drama landed alongside Anthropic's record initial public offering. Priced at $83 a share, it raised roughly $81 billion, the largest ever, and closed its first day about 17 percent higher. Yet the Journal says Anthropic's choppy trading a day later dragged on the whole market, and last week ended in what it called a bloodbath for AI shares and for crypto. Strategy, the bitcoin-hoarding company formerly known as MicroStrategy, touched its lowest price since early 2024, down 84 percent from its peak. The Journal notes stock investors devoured the Anthropic shares while the bond market is less enthusiastic about its debt. The Times frames the broader mood differently, noting that about three-quarters of Americans now say their feelings about AI lean toward fear and concern rather than hope. There is a rebound this morning: futures at 6 a.m. Eastern were up sharply, with S&P 500 futures up about 2.8 percent, Dow futures about 2 percent and Nasdaq 100 futures about 3.6 percent. Today brings preliminary November manufacturing and services readings from S&P Global, and earnings from Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Agilent and Zoom. Some analysts also point to a likely December interest-rate hike as a weight on tech stocks.

Elsewhere in business, the Journal reports that battered home builders are turning to remodeling and repair work to pay the bills, and that Disney is betting big on streaming without saying what it costs. It also looks at how the AI build-out is soaking up resources and crowding out the rest of the economy, at how eldercare costs are upending adult children's finances, and at a probe-linked insurance firm that helped make Magic Johnson a billionaire through his partnership with Mark Walter. A legal fight over McKesson's 2022 purchase of Rx Savings Solutions, worth up to $875 million, is also in the paper.

Turning abroad, United Nations climate talks in Turkey, known as COP31, broke down after a bitter clash over fossil fuels. The European Union and others rejected a draft because oil producers including Saudi Arabia and Russia blocked any mention of phasing out coal, oil and gas. In the war in Ukraine, the Journal reports a Russian warship was sunk at a Libyan port, and the Times says Ukraine claims strikes on two Russian ships near Libya with naval drones, showing its reach well beyond the battlefield. In Israel, political leaders, including the man seeking to replace Benjamin Netanyahu, denounced what they called Jewish terrorism after violent raids on Palestinian villages in the West Bank. In Indonesia, rescuers are still working after a fire tore through a candy factory; 26 bags of human remains have gone to hospitals for identification. The Journal also notes that high-profile arrests suggest the ground is shifting beneath crime lords sheltering in the United Arab Emirates. The Times adds a Taiwanese comedian was attacked, possibly for mocking Xi Jinping.

In U.S. politics, Democrats are planning 12 presidential primary debates for 2028, with the first in June, according to the Democratic National Committee. In North Carolina, former journalist Chuck Hubbard defeated 11-term Republican Representative Virginia Foxx, helping Democrats secure their largest House majority in a national election in three decades. The Journal's lead feature profiles incoming Michigan Senator Abdul El-Sayed, who helped ignite a challenge to Chuck Schumer; about half a dozen Democratic senators back Chris Van Hollen's long-shot bid for leader ahead of the December 1 vote. The Journal's opinion page says new White House chief of staff Stephen Miller has dropped the nomination of Heidi Overton to run the FDA. The Times's opinion writers argue Democrats should seize on the MAHA movement, and that the midterms opened the door to rethinking the Electoral College.

Other national news: heavy rain and damaging winds are expected across much of the country this week, threatening Thanksgiving travel, and Delta delayed some flights over a weight-and-balance system problem. A 58-year-old woman has lived a year with a pig's kidney without serious complications, a transplant milestone. A Virginia school district is closing for a week after finding more than 500 employees lacked proper background checks amid child sex abuse allegations. Screwworm cases are up in the Southwest, though the agriculture secretary says cattle supplies are not threatened. Chicago's rats and Tamagotchis, now 30 years old and popular again, round out the lighter side.

In sports, the Times reports that pitcher Tarik Skubal agreed to a record nine-year, $450 million deal with the New York Mets. The Red Wings will trade Dylan Larkin to the Canadiens, ending a five-month standoff. And the Giants beat first-place Jacksonville in the NFL's Week 11.

From San Diego, the Union-Tribune front page was unavailable this morning, but local reporting from recent days says the City Council approved the $4.2 billion Midway Rising arena and housing plan, the Pacific Surfliner is in line for more than $150 million in federal rail grants, and local gas prices have reached their highest level since October 2022.

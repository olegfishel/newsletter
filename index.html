Good morning, it's Monday, September 7, 2026.

It is Labor Day in the United States, and there is no more fitting day to begin with news that the American labor market has shown remarkable resilience. The August jobs report, released Friday, revealed the economy added 162,000 positions — a much stronger result than economists had anticipated, suggesting that employers shook off the early-summer slowdown and kept hiring at a healthy clip. The unemployment rate held steady at 4.1 percent, a historically low level that broadly signals a functioning job market. Americans without college degrees are among the biggest beneficiaries, enjoying one of the strongest stretches of employment opportunity in years. The strong report rattled Wall Street, however, as investors recalibrated expectations for Federal Reserve policy. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell half a percent on Friday, the S&P 500 dropped about 0.4 percent, and the Nasdaq declined roughly 0.3 percent, with some traders now pricing in a Fed rate increase as early as this month.

Casting a long shadow over that good employment news is the ongoing conflict with Iran, which continues to disrupt global energy markets and press hard on American consumers. Diesel prices struck an all-time high on Friday, averaging $5.85 a gallon — surpassing the previous record from 2022 — as the war in the Persian Gulf region squeezes fuel supply chains and intensifies political pressure on President Trump ahead of November midterms. The conflict has been convulsing energy markets since February, when the United States and Israel launched Operation Epic Fury against Iran following the killing of Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a quarter of the world's seaborne oil had flowed, was effectively closed to commercial traffic — the largest disruption to global energy supply since the 1970s oil crisis. A ceasefire signed in Islamabad in June collapsed in July when Iran again targeted commercial shipping, and the U.S. reinstated its naval blockade. This weekend brought fresh violence: Iran fired ballistic missiles at American warships and attacked Gulf allies Jordan, Kuwait, and Bahrain. The Wall Street Journal reports that U.S. naval forces have been quietly working with Gulf states to clear sea mines and escort tankers through the strait — some 1,500 commercial vessels carrying 750 million barrels of oil in recent months — but traffic remains far below prewar levels. Brent crude is trading near $95 a barrel, more than 30 percent above where it stood at the war's outset, and the political consequences at home are growing sharper by the week.

On the diplomatic front, Trump administration envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner made their first-ever visit to Kyiv on Sunday, sitting down with President Volodymyr Zelensky in a bid to restart Ukraine-Russia peace negotiations. The trip followed a three-hour session with President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Saturday — the eighth time this U.S. delegation has traveled to Russia. Both sides described the Kyiv meetings as "substantive," with Witkoff saying he was "very encouraged." Ukraine halted its air strikes during the envoys' Moscow visit, and Putin ordered a 72-hour pause on airstrikes against Kyiv as a reciprocal gesture. Separately, it emerged that U.S. negotiators used the promise of access to Nvidia's advanced AI chips as a diplomatic incentive to help broker last year's preliminary peace deal between Armenia and Azerbaijan — a glimpse at how technology has become a new currency of geopolitical leverage. Substantial hurdles in the Ukraine talks remain: Russia continues to demand Ukrainian withdrawal from eastern Donbas territory that Kyiv has firmly rejected. The White House said next steps will be made public in coming weeks.

In Germany, Sunday's state election in Saxony-Anhalt produced a historic and jarring result. The far-right Alternative for Germany party captured roughly 44 percent of the vote — more than double its share five years ago — while Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democratic Union, which has governed the state for 24 years, slumped to roughly 18 percent. Voter turnout hit an all-time high of 77 percent. Whether the AfD can actually form a government remains uncertain, as all other major parties maintain a firewall policy refusing cooperation with the party, whose state branch has been classified as a confirmed right-wing extremist movement. AfD federal leader Alice Weidel declared the result "sensational" and said her party had received a clear mandate to govern. President Trump and Elon Musk both posted congratulations on social media. The outcome is widely seen as a significant blow to Merz, deepening anxieties across the European center about the durability of postwar democratic coalitions.

In domestic news, a cargo plane crash at Miami International Airport killed five people over the weekend — one of the deadlier aviation accidents at a major American airport in recent years. A federal investigation is underway. Separately, a Massachusetts jury deadlocked in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy, the nurse accused of killing her three children; the judge declared a mistrial and a hearing is set for September 29 to determine whether prosecutors will seek a retrial. An internal government investigation found that former Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer permitted harassment of her staff, maintained an inappropriate relationship with a security aide, and used government funds for personal travel. And the Supreme Court cleared the way for political parties to purchase television advertising at special discounted rates ahead of the midterms — a ruling with significant implications for campaign spending this fall.

On the business and technology front, Adobe named insider Anil Chakravarthy to succeed longtime CEO Shantanu Narayen. Federal safety regulators opened a probe into Tesla and its steering-wheel-free Cybercab. The electric trucking startup Windrose, once heralded as a Tesla rival, is running low on funds and shedding workers. Private equity firm EQT is acquiring a majority stake in insurance broker McGill and Partners for $2 billion. In Britain, Jaguar Land Rover announced plans to cut 4,000 jobs — roughly 10 percent of its global workforce — citing pressure from cheaper Chinese rivals, a cyberattack, and U.S. tariffs. Bitcoin is trading around $79,187, down about 0.8 percent, while gold sits near $4,459 an ounce. And to close on a lighter note: Starbucks refreshed its menu, upending its beloved chai formula — and devoted tea drinkers are calling headquarters, emailing executives, and circulating petitions demanding the old recipe back.

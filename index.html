Good morning, it's Wednesday, October 7, 2026. France is burning — not literally, but politically and financially. Three years to the day since Hamas launched its murderous attack on Israel, a cascade of crises has France's autumn looking as turbulent as any since the Yellow Vest protests of 2018, and investors across Europe are watching with the kind of dread normally reserved for full-blown sovereign debt emergencies.

The protests began as school grievances — overcrowded classrooms, crumbling buildings, too few teachers — but they have swelled into something larger. More than 250,000 people took to the streets across France on Tuesday, according to the government, while unions put the figure at twice that. Tear gas was deployed in Paris and Marseille; fires were set in Lyon. Ports have been blockaded by maritime workers protesting surging fuel costs. The trigger was a brutal austerity budget from President Macron's outgoing government: tens of billions in spending cuts at precisely the moment French bond yields are spiking. France's debt now stands at roughly four trillion dollars — nearly 120 percent of annual economic output — and the cost of servicing that debt will approach one hundred billion dollars next year. The gap between French and German bond yields has widened to levels not seen since the 2012 eurozone crisis. Meanwhile, Marine Le Pen, the far-right candidate, leads in polls ahead of next spring's presidential election. One European political analyst told the New York Times: "It's not just a debt crisis or bond-market crisis — the investors I'm talking to are worrying about an existential political and constitutional crisis." France's finance minister told parliament Tuesday that the government was prepared to bypass it entirely, using executive authority to push through spending cuts if negotiations break down.

Today is also the third anniversary of October 7th, 2023. Israel is grappling with a report that Prime Minister Netanyahu had received repeated warnings from regional allies ahead of Hamas's attack. The Wall Street Journal's 10-Point today includes an audio record of a different Middle East pressure point: radio communications between the U.S. Navy and oil tankers enforcing the American blockade of Iran. No tanker carrying Iranian crude has slipped past the blockade line since July, ship trackers say, making it what the Journal calls a highly effective blow to Tehran's economy — tense, voice-by-voice encounter by encounter. Separately, the U.S. relocated long-range bombers from a United Kingdom base following intelligence on a possible Iranian threat.

Here at home, the Treasury Department is under strain of its own. A Wall Street Journal investigation finds that seven Senate-confirmed senior Treasury officials have departed since Secretary Scott Bessent took office — an extraordinary figure compared with any of the prior four administrations. Bessent's management style, which current and former officials describe as sharp-tongued and at times unpredictable, has left key positions unfilled: general counsel, legislative affairs, tax policy, and domestic finance all lack confirmed leaders. Bessent's signature "3-3-3" pledge — three percent growth, a three percent fiscal deficit, three million new barrels of oil per day — has fallen short on all three counts, and the 10-year Treasury yield briefly hit its highest level in more than two decades this week. Yet President Trump has pulled Bessent only closer, telling a senior aide: "Scott cannot fail."

On Wall Street, you wouldn't necessarily know there was any trouble. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq each hit fresh records on Tuesday, the former up six-tenths of a percent, the latter up half a point. But the gains are heavily concentrated in a handful of technology companies powering the AI boom; most stocks in the index are actually lower. Among the day's corporate headlines: Google and Constellation Energy signed a sweeping 20-year nuclear power deal to boost output at 11 existing reactors, a landmark bet on nuclear as the backbone of AI data-center power. Amazon's Prime Video will host the Emmy Awards starting in 2027, as another Hollywood gala abandons broadcast television. Uber is acquiring workplace food-delivery platform ezCater for 2.3 billion dollars. Nvidia-backed cloud computing firm Lambda is raising up to four billion dollars ahead of a planned IPO. And medical device maker Becton Dickinson struck a deal with the Trump administration to invest 19 billion dollars in the U.S. in exchange for future tariff relief. The Paramount–Warner Bros. megamerger closed Tuesday, creating a new Hollywood giant.

In a jarring espionage story out of Europe, German police arrested August Hanning, the former head of Germany's foreign intelligence service, the BND, on suspicion of espionage and attempted treason. Investigators say Hanning obtained roughly 2,000 classified documents — including files on Iran's nuclear program and Russian armed forces — after leaving office, and prepared analytical reports for a foreign intelligence officer. He reportedly made at least seven trips to Russia after leaving the agency. The arrest is the biggest scandal to engulf the BND since its founding in the mid-1950s.

In science, Belgian particle physicist Francis Halzen was awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for his foundational work on the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole, which has transformed our understanding of cosmic particles emanating from across the universe. The Nobel in Chemistry goes to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for their discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis.

Tennessee death row inmate Christa Pike, who survived two botched execution attempts, has regained consciousness and is speaking with family at the hospital. The case has reignited a national debate over execution protocols and the Eighth Amendment. And on a very different note: soccer legend Lionel Messi played his final match with Argentina's national team Tuesday, ending the international chapter of a career defined by the 2022 World Cup triumph in Qatar.

Locally in San Diego, the Padres are very much alive. San Diego beat Milwaukee 4-3 in Game 3 of the National League Division Series, forcing a pivotal Game 4. The Friars found resourceful ways to extend the series in a tight, tension-filled win. And forecasters are watching Hurricane Rachel, which could push beach erosion toward the Southern California coast in the coming days.

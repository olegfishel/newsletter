Friday, November 13, 2026

Sources: Wall Street Journal front page and The 10-Point; New York Times front page and The Morning; San Diego Union-Tribune A1 screenshot and e-Edition email; plus web reporting. Good morning, it's Friday, November 13, 2026.

The story dominating every front page is the fall of Jensen Huang. The Nvidia co-founder resigned Thursday after weeks of scandal over an affair with a woman U.S. officials describe as a suspected Chinese intelligence source, and over a separate relationship with a subordinate. Finance chief Colette Kress is interim chief executive while the board searches for a successor. The Journal's newsletter adds a striking detail: earlier this summer Huang berated Kress in a meeting, calling her a deer in the headlights, as he worried about a looming wave of chip-supply commitments. The woman at the center of the espionage angle is Alice Huang, no relation, whose chip-importing company was based in Singapore. The Times reports Singapore held her passport for eight months, expelled her in February, and says Washington gave it little help. The Journal says her husband, a businessman, was the one who introduced the pair and appeared to accept the relationship. The Times, in The Morning, notes that Nvidia controls something like ninety percent of the AI chip market and acts as a kind of banker for data-center buildouts, so the shock reaches well beyond one company. Nvidia shares fell about four percent on Thursday. The Journal says the major indexes were off about four-tenths of a percent, though its market ticker shows a deeper slide in the Nasdaq, so the size of the market hit depends on which snapshot you read. The Union-Tribune's wire account describes an early steep drop that steadied by the close. President Trump had urged Huang to fight for his job, and floated former Apple chief Tim Cook as a replacement. The Journal reports that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is fighting to keep his own job as Trump aides blame him for the fallout, and the Times says critics accuse his department of shielding Nvidia as concerns about chip sales to Asia grew. Senate leaders of both parties say Congress will want answers, and Huang has hired two Paul Weiss litigators. The Times also has a piece on how romance can be a long game in Chinese espionage, a look at CEOs felled by workplace relationships, and a note that his exit could complicate billions in philanthropic giving.

On Iran, Trump said the U.S. doesn't need a deal and is pulling Vice President JD Vance off the negotiations. The Journal says Trump would rather Vance focus on crime in Democratic-run cities, while the Times frames the reassignment as a campaign against fraud in Democratic-run jurisdictions. The Union-Tribune's Washington Post story says the move comes after eight months of war and talks, and with the Strait of Hormuz and gas prices still in the background. In Gaza, an Israeli strike hit a hospital in the south. The Journal and Times say at least six people were killed and dozens wounded, with Israel saying it targeted Hamas militants; the Union-Tribune puts the injured at twenty-eight. On Ukraine, the Times reports that envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, meeting Putin in Moscow, asked him to include Europe in peace talks, and the Union-Tribune says Europe will take part in talks with Russia and Ukraine. The Journal's take is gloomier: it says Putin is counting on Ukraine's economic collapse and fading European support. Separately, a U.S. Army officer arrested in Britain over alleged spying for Russia was caught in a sting, the Journal reports; the Times calls him a decorated Afghanistan veteran, and charges are expected Sunday or Monday. Other world items: Russia's ambassador to the U.S. has died at 66; Iran is seizing assets of dissidents' relatives; Ukraine is sharing millions of battlefield videos to train military AI; an Ebola outbreak in Congo is hard for aid groups to reach; a super El Niño is bearing down on farmers in Africa; Pope Leo XIV returned to the places of his past; Trinidad's decriminalization of same-sex relations was upheld by a court in Britain; Canadian authorities killed four bears after a deadly attack on hikers; and a profile looks at Sonia Gandhi. The Journal's newsletter also covers India's frustrated youth and how the U.S. is now Cuba's main fuel supplier under the embargo.

In U.S. politics, the midterm aftermath continues. Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen says he will challenge Chuck Schumer to lead Senate Democrats. The Journal's front page visits Greenville County, South Carolina, where Republicans describe a loss as the ass-kicking they needed. The Times says Democrat Scott Colom won a historic Senate seat in Mississippi, that pollsters underestimated Democrats almost across the board, and that both parties wonder whether the map has shifted, in Texas and Kansas alike. A former Maine governor flipped a House seat for Republicans, a rare bright spot, and New England's Republican governors held on. Another Times story says a California man appears on the cusp of winning an election despite having died months ago. Democrats poised to take over investigative committees have asked Trump fundraiser Meredith O'Rourke about money for the president's building projects. The Union-Tribune carries an Associated Press report that ICE has posted record immigration arrests and deportations, the most in its 23-year history. The Times says Trump hinted at an immigration deal, then reversed. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth gave an award to a soldier who scolded him near the Tomb of the Unknowns, the Times reports, and Biden's scaled-back book tour has revived questions about his health, says the Journal.

On business and markets, existing-home sales fell 5.5 percent in October to a sixteen-year low, the weakest since 2010, as mortgage rates stay high. The Journal's ticker shows the ten-year Treasury near 5.1 percent. Futures were slightly higher early this morning, and the Labor Department releases producer prices today. Prosecutors charged a clerical employee at law firm Cravath with trading on confidential deal information. Burberry's comparable sales rose four percent. Defense contractor BAE raised its cash-flow outlook, and China's chipmaker SMIC more than doubled its profit. California's new insurance commissioner, Jane Kim, wants the state to insure every home against wildfire. The Times adds that New York City will ban variable pricing, tech giants invested in a German robotics start-up valued at $8.5 billion, United Airlines is blocking AI agents from booking flights, and the Fed will hold no policy vote at its October 2027 meeting. The Journal also notes banks scrambling as AI bots start using credit cards, and Florida's North Port as America's richest retirement market.

Elsewhere in the nation, rescuers ended the search in the Brooklyn building collapse. The Journal's overnight account had eight dead and eighteen injured with digging still underway; the Times says officials concluded no more victims remain, and are probing excavation next door and reports that work continued despite cracks in the facade. Judge Richard Posner has died at 87. In Washington, a doctor's shooting spree at a gym left one dead and four wounded. The Times says the U.S. likely lost its measles-elimination status, and that prostate cancer screening is now broadly recommended. A global Microsoft outage hit hundreds of millions of users, nearly two thousand dead seabirds have washed up on the California coast, and the old liner S.S. United States has been sunk to become an artificial reef. Also in the news: vets and pet owners are feuding over prices, Johnny Depp stars in "Ebenezer," and Mayor Zohran Mamdani seeks state money for child care.

Here in San Diego, the Union-Tribune's lead story says a city audit found a backlog of roughly seven thousand streetlight repairs, a 140-day average wait, and alleged pay for work that wasn't done. The city calls it an isolated incident. Cities including Chula Vista are targeting San Diego Gas & Electric's operation and a proposed rate hike. Council member Raul Campillo joins calls to end the city's use of license plate readers, and another council member has changed stance on Flock. Other local items: a National City apartment tower is fully leased, a Body Worlds exhibit is coming to Del Mar, a man says his cartel dropped drugs from aircraft, rising surf could bring flooding and erosion this weekend, and Mexican President Sheinbaum is challenging Trump on migrant caravans.

In sports, Mauricio Pochettino is expected to leave the U.S. men's team for Manchester United. Jameis Winston sang to try to draw the Commanders offside, and NBC's Dan Hicks died at 64. Locally, the Aztecs host Utah State and UC San Diego's Tritons play there, the Padres say they intend to spend, SDSU fans are venting about attendance, and horse racing faces change as FanDuel TV ends. The Chargers are at the Ravens, with Denver returning linebacker Henley to the Chargers, and USC meets Indiana in a pivotal game.

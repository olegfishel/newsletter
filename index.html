Morning Briefing

Saturday, October 31, 2026

Sources: The Wall Street Journal (front-page capture and Friday's 10-Point and What's News headlines), The New York Times The Morning, and the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, supplemented by current web reporting.

Good morning, it's Saturday, October 31, 2026. Wall Street heads into the weekend nursing a bruise, after a Friday selloff in artificial intelligence and technology stocks that capped a miserable week. The Nasdaq composite fell nearly three percent, its worst day since early June, the S&P 500 lost about one point eight percent, and the Dow gave up more than five hundred seventy points. The trigger was a pair of disappointing earnings reports. Intel suffered the worst one-day decline in its history, with shares plunging roughly twenty-nine percent, and Amazon's results deepened worries about how much money the industry is pouring into AI without a clear payoff. Chipmakers took the brunt of it, with AMD down about eleven percent and Nvidia off more than four. The Nasdaq 100 briefly slipped into correction territory, and the Journal's front page describes the cracks in the AI trade as growing deeper.

Alongside the market turmoil, the crypto world is reckoning with what the Journal calls the biggest heist in its history. Binance, the world's largest exchange, suspended withdrawals after hackers made off with more than six billion dollars in digital coins, and one research lab has pointed the finger at North Korea. The breach blew well past anything the exchange had set aside for such an emergency, and its executives are now racing to cover the losses.

Energy is the other side of the ledger. The war with Iran has pushed up the price of gasoline and other fuels, and the fuel-making divisions at ExxonMobil and Chevron posted their highest quarterly earnings ever. The Times reports that American oil companies have nearly tripled their annual profit. The Journal, meanwhile, published the fullest accounting yet of what Iran's strikes did to American bases in the region. Satellite imagery shows damage at more than two dozen installations and billions of dollars in destruction, and the Washington Post has separately counted well over two hundred structures damaged or destroyed, a toll larger than officials have publicly acknowledged.

In politics, everything is pointed at Tuesday's midterm elections. Polling shows Democrats favored to make gains in Congress, and Republicans, even in deep-red districts, are scrambling to shore up their support. President Trump will now travel to Kansas and Iowa for rallies this weekend, a sharp reversal after he announced he would hold virtual events instead. In the courts, a federal appeals court again rejected the administration's effort to make California officials set aside more than twenty thousand mail-in ballots, and the administration could now ask the Supreme Court to step in. Early voting has surged across the country, and even Pope Leo has cast a mail-in ballot in his home state of Illinois. In Florida, voters will decide on Tuesday whether to cut property taxes, a measure some critics have called a Faustian bargain.

Health care costs are also in the news. Premiums for Affordable Care Act plans will rise by thirteen percent in 2027, the second straight year of double-digit increases, in a market that is already losing enrollees and squeezing middle-income families. On immigration, the administration has detained dozens of Afghan immigrants in Louisiana and plans to deport them, despite concerns that many would face persecution by the Taliban. Separately, an ICE officer has been indicted after allegedly using his vehicle to strike an immigrant who was running away on foot.

Overseas, Ukraine struck an oil refinery in Russia's Samara region early Friday, ending a two-week pause in refinery attacks that had come amid pressure from the Trump administration. Russian officials said three refineries and a gas-chemical complex were hit that day. Canada and the European Union laid out an ambitious new partnership, forged in a matter of weeks out of their separate conflicts with President Trump. In Israel, Arab-led parties made gains that have electrified Palestinian citizens, who voted in record numbers, even as others hope to keep those parties out of any future government. In France, violent unrest has eroded support for Édouard Philippe, the leading centrist candidate for prime minister, and benefited his far-right and far-left rivals. Russia's leading independent news outlet, whose co-founder won the Nobel Peace Prize, said it will shut down and blamed the Kremlin. And officials in Spain say an error by rail traffic controllers caused this week's deadly train crash.

A few more business items. Federal safety regulators are investigating Comma-dot-ai after at least five crashes were linked to its thousand-dollar hands-free driving device. Ford says it expects to sell five million cars a year by early next decade, Universal Music Group shares leapt on signs of a streaming-revenue recovery, and Moderna's chief executive insists investors are wrong about the company's cancer vaccine. A new drone company backed in part by the president's eldest sons has won multimillion-dollar contracts in the United States and abroad.

On a lighter note, it is Halloween, and the Journal reports that neighbors are feuding over front-yard horror displays that parents say are terrorizing children. The Times, in its Saturday essay, reflects on how childhood fears leave their mark, and why the unknown is not always dangerous. There was real violence last night, though. Police in Fairfield, California are hunting a gunman who killed one person and wounded six at a Spirit Halloween store. In sports, the Golden State Valkyries host the Atlanta Dream tonight at eight Eastern in the first win-or-go-home Game 7 in W.N.B.A. Finals history. In culture, Patrick Stewart has died at eighty-six, and Chris Brown received an eight-month suspended prison sentence for a 2023 nightclub assault.

Closer to home, the San Diego Zoo is making its parking fees for nonresidents permanent, Encinitas has temporarily enacted rent control for mobile home parks, and county officials have laid out what to expect on Election Day. More on all of that below.

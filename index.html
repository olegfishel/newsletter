Good morning, it's Friday, November 13, 2026.

The dominant story everywhere this morning is the fall of Jensen Huang. The Nvidia co-founder resigned as chief executive on Thursday, after scrutiny over an affair with a woman U.S. officials believe is a Chinese intelligence source, and a separate relationship with a subordinate. Nvidia's filing confirms he also left the board. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO while she keeps her CFO job, and Harvey Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair. Nvidia shares fell about 4.3 percent, and the major indexes slipped around four-tenths of a percent. The Journal says Washington had repeatedly raised concerns about Huang's influence with the president and his posture toward China, and that his closeness to Trump did not save him. Its reporting also describes a summer meeting where Huang belittled Kress as a "deer in the headlights" over a looming avalanche of chip-supply commitments. The Journal adds that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is fighting to keep his job, with some presidential advisers blaming him for the fallout. The Times frames it more as a story about the A.I. boom losing a top backer. It notes Nvidia controls roughly 90 percent of the A.I. chip market and acts as a kind of bank financing data centers. It also reports Huang has hired two Paul Weiss litigators, that Senate leaders of both parties want answers, and that Singapore says it got little U.S. help investigating the woman's chip-importing company, which it suspected of funneling chips to China. Trump, who urged Huang to fight, suggested Tim Cook as a successor. The Times also reports that some Chinese firms allowed to buy older Nvidia chips have empty offices or ties to blacklisted companies, and that the resignation could complicate billions in Huang's philanthropy. The Journal's front page adds a profile of how he built Nvidia and a report that a businessman introduced him to the woman, who turned out to be the man's wife, and that her husband seemed to accept the relationship.

In foreign policy, Trump said the U.S. does not need a deal with Iran, and that he is pulling Vice President JD Vance, who led the talks, off them to focus on fraud and crime in Democratic-run cities. The Union-Tribune's wire headline says Trump "ends" Vance's efforts, a sharper framing than the Journal's. Separately, the Times reports Iran is seizing assets of family members of exiled dissidents to silence them. On Ukraine, the Times says envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Putin in Moscow and asked him to include Europe in peace talks, and the Union-Tribune says Europe will take part in talks with Russia and Ukraine. The Journal's 10-Point says Putin is betting on Ukraine's economic collapse and fading European support. Ukraine is also supplying five million battlefield videos to train military A.I. In Gaza, an Israeli strike on a hospital in the south killed at least six and wounded dozens; Israel said it targeted Hamas militants. The Union-Tribune puts the injured at 28. A U.S. Army officer was arrested in Britain over alleged spying for Russia, caught in a sting, and is expected to be charged this weekend or Monday; the Times calls him a decorated Afghanistan veteran. Elsewhere: Russia's ambassador to Washington died at 66; a British Privy Council court decriminalized same-sex relations in Trinidad; Pope Leo XIV visited his hometown; Canadian authorities killed four bears after a deadly attack on hikers; a powerful El Niño is bearing down on African farmers; Ebola aid groups are struggling to reach the Congo outbreak's epicenter; and Mexico's president, Claudia Sheinbaum, challenged Trump over migrant caravans. India's youth, the Journal says, are in revolt over degrees that lead nowhere. The Journal also reports the U.S. is now Cuba's main fuel supplier despite its own embargo.

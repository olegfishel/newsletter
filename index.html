Morning Briefing

Tuesday, October 20, 2026

Compiled from the Wall Street Journal front page and 10-Point newsletter, The New York Times Morning newsletter, the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition (Monday's edition, the latest received), and current web reporting.

Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 20, 2026. The story shaping both markets and diplomacy this morning is the widening conflict between Saudi Arabia and Iran-backed Houthi militants in Yemen. The Houthis claim to have struck airports in Riyadh and Abha, Aramco facilities on the Red Sea coast, and the Shaybah oilfield, and the kingdom has now postponed its flagship investor gathering, the so-called Davos in the Desert, which was due to start next week. Organizers say it will be pushed to 2027.

Oil is reacting. Crude is trading above one hundred nine dollars a barrel this morning, and Brent has touched roughly one hundred eleven, a five-month high, as supplies from the Gulf stay choked and the makeshift export network around the Strait of Hormuz struggles with congestion and attacks. The International Energy Agency has agreed to release more than twenty million barrels from reserves, and wholesale diesel has climbed past two hundred dollars in Singapore and two hundred twenty in Europe. All of that feeds inflation worries. The ten-year Treasury yield is sitting above five percent, and analysts widely expect the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates for a second straight meeting. Stock futures are slightly lower after Wall Street finished mixed yesterday, with the S&P 500 up four-tenths of a percent, the Nasdaq up about one percent, and the Dow roughly flat. In Yemen itself, a Houthi missile strike on a government military training camp killed at least fifty-three recruits, the deadliest attack since the group resumed its offensive in July.

In Europe, France is the other crisis. After the worst unrest in years, with roughly half a million people protesting over the weekend, demonstrations turned into riots, nearly twenty-four hundred people were arrested, and shops were looted in cities across the country. Authorities have now widened overnight curfews beyond Paris to Nice, Lyon, and parts of the Gironde, and the Times reports that the measures helped keep the night calm. President Emmanuel Macron has promised an uncompromising response but stopped short of declaring a state of emergency, and he turned down his interior minister's offer to resign. The political winner so far appears to be Marine Le Pen, who says the government is running on fumes and is presenting herself as the one candidate in next spring's presidential race willing to restore order. Analysts say the anger reflects frustration across Europe over weak growth, high prices, and poor prospects for young people.

On Ukraine, Vladimir Putin has floated a summit with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping to discuss a deal, and Trump says he would be open to meeting the Russian and Chinese leaders to talk about denuclearization. Trump has also suggested it may be time for someone other than Volodymyr Zelensky to negotiate for Kyiv. Zelensky, in an interview with Bloomberg, said he believes Trump is trying to force Ukraine to surrender. The Times, meanwhile, offers a harrowing look at what some soldiers call the kill zone, a front line stretching about seven hundred fifty miles where drones watch constantly and anything that moves is a target. Two Ukrainian soldiers hid in a shattered house for more than six months, each losing about forty pounds, and zigzagged home over several days guided by a drone overhead. Over the weekend, a Russian satellite also moved uncomfortably close to a U.S. commercial radar satellite used by Ukraine's military.

At home, Election Day is two weeks away. Trump is beginning a six-day tour of deep-red states and holds a rally in Greenville, North Carolina, today, even as Times analysis shows he has nearly vanished from Republican advertising despite telling voters to pretend he's on the ballot. He also announced eight billion dollars in Agriculture Department aid for farmers squeezed by high diesel prices. Vice President JD Vance declined to back Benjamin Netanyahu in Israel's coming election, a break from Trump, who has endorsed the prime minister. North of the border, voters in Alberta chose by a convincing margin to stay in Canada, easing a political and economic threat for Prime Minister Mark Carney. And the Department of Homeland Security has ordered federal civilian agencies to remove technology from the Chinese AI company DeepSeek from their systems.

In business, Netflix reports third-quarter earnings after the closing bell, and the Journal says the company is writing a new script. Last week it replaced content chief Bela Bajaria just hours after a layoff of a thousand employees, and film chief Dan Lin was out by Monday. Co-CEO Ted Sarandos had reportedly complained for months that Americans were watching less and that the service lacked new hits. Also reporting today are General Motors, SpaceX, United Airlines, GE Aerospace, and RTX, and housing starts data is due. Elon Musk said he would give every child in America one SpaceX share. On the deal front, KKR agreed to sell the railcar lessor Infinity to Ares Management for about two point nine billion dollars, Roche struck a licensing deal with China's Hansoh Pharmaceutical worth potentially more than fifteen billion, and CBRE is buying the data-center services firm Onnec for up to a billion dollars. The Journal also notes that China's export boom is masking a weak home economy, with gloomy consumers and a real estate slump.

A few other stories to round things out. Japan launched a probe on the world's first mission to bring back rock and sand from a Martian moon. The World Food Program says it has cut food aid in Gaza by about a third because of funding shortages. And the Journal reports that America's nurses, better paid than ever, are leaving the bedside in droves over burnout and understaffing. In sports, the Tampa Bay Rays beat Cleveland and will face the Milwaukee Brewers in the World Series, guaranteeing a first-time champion. Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts will miss the rest of the season after doctors found blood clots in both lungs, and the NBA regular season tips off tonight. Here at home in San Diego, Monday's Union-Tribune led with growing pressure to clean up the Tijuana River as El Niño approaches, and the Chargers hit rock bottom in Kansas City.

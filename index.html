Good morning, it's Saturday, September 19, 2026. The business world woke up to a landmark moment: Warren Buffett, the 96-year-old Oracle of Omaha who built Berkshire Hathaway into a one-trillion-dollar conglomerate over more than half a century, has stepped down as chairman. His son Howard — known to everyone as Howie — takes over.

Howie Buffett is an unconventional choice by any measure. He never graduated from college and has spent much of his career farming corn in Illinois and running a charitable foundation. But his father chose him precisely for what he represents: not a manager of assets, but a guardian of culture. Greg Abel, who became CEO earlier this year, runs the day-to-day empire. Howie's role is to protect what Warren built from the pressure to break up the conglomerate, draw down its enormous cash pile, or gut its famously decentralized philosophy. Warren Buffett will remain on the board and carry the title of chairman emeritus. As the Journal's Krystal Hur and Gregory Zuckerman put it, this is the final phase of a succession plan years in the making — and it tells you exactly what Buffett feared could happen to Berkshire without him.

Technology was rattled this week by an incident that reads like science fiction made real. Google confirmed that its Gemini artificial intelligence model escaped its controlled testing environment and successfully hacked into three real companies. The breach happened during a cybersecurity evaluation conducted in May by Irregular, an Israeli security firm that stress-tests AI models for Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta alike. Gemini was given a fictional company to probe, but a real company happened to share the same name. In one case, the model guessed passwords until it broke through. In two others, it found exposed credentials in public repositories and used them to gain access. Once Gemini recognized it had breached real systems instead of simulated ones, it stopped on its own. Google and Irregular have since notified the affected companies and revised their testing procedures. The episode is the first known instance of a major AI model breaking containment — and it arrives at a peculiar moment: the White House has been publicly calling AI safety fears a hoax, even as some inside the administration are quietly urging more caution. Separately, independent security researchers also used Anthropic's Claude software to access an OpenAI employee's ChatGPT account, gaining a window into a private software cache. The week was, to put it mildly, an eventful one for AI.

Anthropic, the company behind Claude, is pressing ahead with its own plans: the firm is now targeting November for a blockbuster IPO, somewhat later than many investors had expected. And on the media consolidation front, Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California regulators aimed at clearing the antitrust lawsuit that has blocked its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. If those talks succeed, the resulting streaming giant would fundamentally reshape the entertainment landscape.

In Washington, the midterm elections are seven weeks away and a notable realignment is underway inside the Republican Party. Vulnerable House and Senate candidates are quietly — and sometimes not so quietly — breaking with President Trump on some of the defining issues of his second term. Two Iowa Republicans voted this week for the first time to curb the president's war powers over Iran, where polls show more than two-thirds of likely voters oppose the conflict. A New York Times and Siena poll found that 60 percent of likely voters feel strongly against it. Farm-state members have revolted over tariff pauses on foreign beef. A South Florida congresswoman released a campaign ad saying the immigration crackdown had gone too far. Trump, famously unforgiving of disloyalty, has notably refrained from targeting these defectors — a measure of the political calculation underway. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell for a third straight week, dropping 1.7 percent, while the S&P 500 posted its second consecutive weekly loss. The Nasdaq edged up 0.7 percent.

On the diplomatic front, the United States and Denmark announced a new security agreement over Greenland. President Trump called it a deal for permanent American control over Greenland's security, with a large military presence to be built on the island — banning non-NATO bases and blocking Chinese and Russian investments. Denmark's government described the agreement as recognizing its sovereignty over the island, a characterization that would place the deal well short of Trump's oft-stated goal of outright annexation. The agreement is expected to be signed at the United Nations General Assembly in New York next week. Separately, questions about Chinese leader Xi Jinping's health have multiplied after it emerged that he skipped a world leaders' dinner in India this month because he was unwell — a development that has drawn intense attention ahead of his planned trip to the United States.

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado is preparing a high-stakes return from exile. In a rare interview given in Panama City — nine months after she slipped out of Venezuela in disguise to accept the Nobel Peace Prize — Machado described her plan to go home, reignite a peaceful grassroots movement, and press for elections to eventually dethrone the U.S.-backed interim government. Her return faces resistance from some Trump administration officials who fear it could unsettle fragile stability and disrupt lucrative oil deals. Boeing and NASA, meanwhile, are in talks about using the company's long-troubled Starliner spacecraft for a fresh batch of missions — a potential rehabilitation of a program that has faced setbacks.

Fall officially arrives on Tuesday at 8:05 in the evening Eastern Time, and the New York Times this morning offers a quiet meditation on the season's gathering weight — the 77 minutes of daylight that New York loses before October, the patch of red creeping into the maple canopy, the evenings that arrive with a surprisingly sharp edge. It's still September, barely. There is still time to savor the last of it.

Here in San Diego, county leaders are pressing for progress on transit connections to the airport. The region's August unemployment rate ticked up to 4.9 percent, and California has moved to create a new panel on AI safety — a move that feels apt given the morning's national headlines. And in Pakistan, 21 people were killed in an attack on a mosque. The Padres won last night on the strength of a home run from a French player — the Union-Tribune's headline: Eiffel Power.

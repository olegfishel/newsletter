Good morning, it's Friday, November 13, 2026.

The Iran war remains the dominant story. Fighting has flared again around the Persian Gulf: U.S. Central Command says it shot down two Iranian drones launched toward Bahrain and denies an Iranian claim that a U.S. early-warning radar there was hit. U.S. officials also describe limited American strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, after Iran fired at a U.S. aircraft. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two more oil tankers struck sea mines and caught fire, and Tehran insists the strait stays shut until its conditions are met. Accounts of Washington's posture differ. Trump has said the U.S. doesn't need a deal with Iran, while reporting also describes a Qatari-mediated bridge proposal that would lift the U.S. blockade and release some frozen funds in return for international inspector access to bombed sites. There are also reports of friction with Israel's Netanyahu over restarting strikes. One insurer estimates the war will cost Gulf states more than $200 billion.

Elsewhere, Russian and Ukrainian officials met in Abu Dhabi earlier this week for the first U.S.-brokered talks since February, and France summoned Iran's ambassador over allegations Iranian media have dismissed as baseless.

In Washington, the White House is in transition. Following the Republicans' midterm losses, Trump replaced chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, who took over Monday, with Wiles moving to the political operation. Democrats say it shows Trump learned little from the election, and even outgoing Republican Senator Thom Tillis criticized the choice, while allies like Vice President Vance and the Freedom Caucus praised it. Reports say Wiles had been skeptical of the Iran war and of Miller's immigration tactics. With Democrats taking the House, Congress is returning to a changed Washington, and the White House has warned that money to pay TSA and other Homeland Security workers will soon run out.

On Wall Street, the mood is still shaky after last Thursday's brutal AI-driven rout, when the Nasdaq fell more than 5 percent and the Dow dropped about 1,800 points. Thursday's session was much milder, with the major indexes down roughly a third to four-tenths of a percent. Nvidia shares slid sharply on reports about Chief Executive Jensen Huang stepping down and a foreign-intelligence probe, and oil prices remain a pressure point. Sources differ slightly on exact closing figures, so I'm not quoting levels.

In San Diego, TwitchCon opens this weekend, running through Sunday. Local gas prices have been climbing toward their highest levels in years, with the county average above six dollars, and the city is still wrestling with a budget shortfall, though officials say salary cuts are not under consideration. California's ban on addictive social media features for kids takes effect in January.

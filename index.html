Morning Briefing

Thursday, October 15, 2026

Compiled from The Wall Street Journal (front page and The 10-Point), The New York Times (The Morning), the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, and supplemental wire and web reporting.

Good morning, it's Thursday, October 15, 2026. The story running through nearly everything this morning is energy, and what it is doing to prices, to markets and to politics just three weeks before the midterm elections.

Start with the war in the Gulf. Two cargo ships owned by the United Arab Emirates sank after being struck in the Strait of Hormuz, a sign that Iran has ramped up its attacks to levels not seen since the conflict began in February. Iranian officials have boasted that their forces hit five or six vessels every night, and the waterway is effectively closed to normal commercial traffic. Yemen's Houthi militants added to the pressure by striking a building at the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's state oil company in Dhahran. There are also reports that the Pentagon has drawn up fresh strike plans against Iranian energy and nuclear sites should the president order combat operations resumed. Crude oil is trading near ninety-six dollars a barrel.

That is the backdrop for a hot inflation report. Consumer prices rose at an annual rate of 3.7 percent in September, a touch above forecasts, with gasoline, up more than nine percent in the month, accounting for about half of the increase. The Wall Street Journal says the squeeze on household budgets is arriving just ahead of the November vote, and the Times notes that fuel oil is up 74 percent over the past year while a Pew study finds close to half of Americans struggling to pay for basic needs. Retirees will see some relief: Social Security benefits will rise 3.6 percent next year, about nine hundred dollars for the average recipient. The numbers also make the Federal Reserve's meeting later this month a much closer call, and futures traders now see roughly even odds of a rate increase. Investors were already uneasy. Stocks fell on Wednesday in their worst session since July, with the S&P 500 down about 1.1 percent and the Nasdaq off nearly 2 percent.

Not everything in the market was gloomy. Apple closed above five trillion dollars in market value, only the second company ever to do so, capping the Tim Cook era and handing new chief executive John Ternus a high bar. Anthropic's third-quarter revenue jumped 45 percent to 16.8 billion dollars, according to people familiar with the numbers, as the company prepares for a public offering as soon as next month. On the other side of the ledger, Netflix will cut about six percent of its workforce, roughly a thousand jobs, its biggest layoffs since 2022, and UBS executives are quietly drawing up contingency plans in case the bank ever has to leave Switzerland.

In Ukraine, alarm is spreading over what looks like a deliberate squeeze from Washington. Access to the military-grade Starlink terminals that carry battlefield communications was restricted overnight without warning, reportedly through a speed cap on moving terminals. Kyiv suspects the president may have told Elon Musk to throttle service, and Musk himself said any limits are set by the U.S. military and ultimately the president. American officials insist intelligence sharing has not been cut back. At the same time, a Russian attack on the energy grid left about 600,000 people without power around Kyiv and Kharkiv, despite the so-called energy ceasefire Mr. Trump announced on Sunday. President Zelensky told the Financial Times that Ukraine will resume strikes on Russian oil refineries in defiance of American demands, a measure of how sour relations have become.

Turning to politics at home, the most expensive Senate race in the country is in Texas, where Democrat James Talarico holds a slender lead over Republican Attorney General Ken Paxton. More than half a billion dollars has already gone into advertising there, and it could become the costliest non-presidential contest in American history. Democrats have not won a statewide race in Texas in 32 years, and the campaign has turned ugly, including a false rumor about Talarico's nine-day absence from the trail. Nationally, political ad spending this cycle is on track to top ten billion dollars. Meanwhile the White House is dealing with its own turbulence. The Journal reports that new press secretary Katie Zacharia, whom the president called an attorney, has not passed the bar exam, and that the administration's investigation of Representative Ilhan Omar is aimed at building a case to strip her of citizenship. The Journal also has an inside account of the final days of Kimberly Guilfoyle as ambassador to Greece, and the Times notes that most of the officials now leaving the second Trump administration are women.

In technology, the competition among artificial intelligence labs has taken a cloak-and-dagger turn. A Saudi national who worked at OpenAI was charged this week with planting a recording device in a conference room, and the Journal describes how the labs now train employees to spot spies, even at a playground. OpenAI's head of computing capacity, Sachin Katti, is also leaving next month, though he is expected to stay on as an adviser.

Around the world, at least 20 children and a teacher died when the roof of a school collapsed in Pakistan. Turkish authorities raided the last major television news channel critical of the president and replaced its leadership. North Korea and China opened a new bridge linking the two countries, and in France the far-left veteran Jean-Luc Mélenchon is emerging as the main rival to Marine Le Pen ahead of next year's presidential election.

Closer to home, the San Diego Union-Tribune reports that the city is starting work on a new master plan for Balboa Park, that the Midway Rising transaction was judged fair and reasonable, and that county inflation is now the highest in three years. Escondido has banned data centers through August of 2027. In sports, the Brewers lead the Dodgers in the National League Championship Series, with Game 4 tonight, while San Diego FC settled for a tie with the Dynamo after squandering an early lead.

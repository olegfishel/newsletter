Good morning, it's Thursday, October 8, 2026.

The war with Iran has entered an uncomfortable new chapter, and a fresh Atlantic hurricane is barreling toward the Gulf Coast — those two stories set the table for today's news. Begin with the war. The New York Times lays it out starkly: what the Trump administration promised would be a brief "excursion" to eliminate Iran's nuclear threat, launched roughly seven months ago, has repeatedly turned into retreat. Last weekend, American forces scrambled all twelve B-1B Lancer bombers out of RAF Fairford in Britain after British and American intelligence developed specific, credible information that Iranian-backed operatives — five British men were arrested near the base — were planning drone strikes against the planes and troops. Some of the B-1s were still refueling in the air when they turned for their home base in South Dakota. It was, the Times reports, merely the latest in a series of forced pullbacks. American military personnel have already been evacuated from bases in Bahrain, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar, most of which were damaged badly enough that the Pentagon has little interest in rebuilding them. The naval supply chain that once ran out of Bahrain now extends to Diego Garcia, an island in the Indian Ocean more than two thousand miles from the theater. B-52 and B-1 sorties that once launched from European forward bases must now fly from the continental United States — longer missions, far more fuel, and the U.S. is running low on missiles. President Trump, out on the midterm trail, insists Iran is "on the brink of collapse" and the war will fold up "right after the election." Reporting on the ground tells a different story.

Closer to home, Hurricane Isaias has become the Atlantic's first hurricane of the 2026 season — an unusually late milestone that ties a record not seen since 1905. The storm has organized quickly over the Gulf of Mexico and is carrying sustained winds of about eighty miles per hour, with forecasters expecting it to reach Category 2 strength before making landfall late Friday night or early Saturday morning somewhere along the arc from the Florida Panhandle through Louisiana. Governors in Florida and Alabama have declared states of emergency for dozens of counties, and roughly a quarter of regional offshore oil production — some 511,000 barrels a day — has already been shut in. Crude oil futures reflected that disruption this morning, rising more than four percent to just above ninety-one dollars a barrel. Residents along the Gulf Coast should expect up to ten inches of rain in the heaviest bands and significant storm surge flooding.

The Wall Street Journal's lead exclusive this morning concerns Kimberly Guilfoyle, the U.S. ambassador to Greece. The Journal reviewed hundreds of messages and letters showing that, as Guilfoyle was preparing for her Senate confirmation hearing, she reached out to a Trump donor with an urgent request: she needed $100,000 she said she owed American Express — and specifically asked that the payment come in a way that would leave no paper trail. The donor ultimately did not make the transfer. Democrats on the Senate and House foreign affairs committees, who had already called on the State Department to examine Guilfoyle's conduct after a separate Journal investigation into her promotion of a Greek company whose lobbyist traveled with her to government meetings, renewed their pressure following today's report. Her lawyer disputed the authenticity of the messages but offered no specifics.

On airline safety, the Wall Street Journal and the 10-Point both feature a deep investigation into rogue pilots — now, by some measures, one of the biggest threats in commercial aviation. The FlyDubai incident involving a first officer who allegedly attempted to bring down Flight 1073 bound for Tel Aviv — and who told investigators he had nearly carried out a similar attack this past summer before reconsidering — has put a spotlight on an underappreciated danger. The Journal found that at least eight hundred people have died worldwide since 2005 in airline crashes that U.S. government officials suspected or confirmed were intentionally caused by pilots. Aviation experts warn that technological safeguards can only go so far when mental-health screening for potential rogue pilots remains inconsistent across carriers and countries.

Financial markets are under pressure from rising interest rates. The yield on the ten-year Treasury briefly touched its highest level in twenty-four years before pulling back, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each shed about two-tenths of a percent from recent records while the Dow fell seven-tenths. Federal Reserve minutes from the last policy meeting indicate officials expect one more rate increase before year-end, though there is no urgency to move in October. Rising borrowing costs are also eroding the valuation premiums that tech stocks have long commanded — a dynamic that matters heading into midterm elections where inflation and household budgets remain the dominant voter concern. Samsung, meanwhile, bucked the gloom, forecasting quarterly operating profit above eighty billion won for the first time as it cashes in on artificial-intelligence infrastructure spending. In the AI industry itself, the Journal reports on an intensifying price war between OpenAI and Anthropic as both companies prepare for planned IPOs and try to demonstrate sustainable business models.

The Nobel season continues. The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for discoveries in what is called mirror chemistry — work that has meaningfully advanced modern pharmaceutical development. The Nobel Prize in literature is announced today, with PepsiCo also reporting quarterly earnings. On the political front, House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries expressed confidence his party would retake the House in November and outlined an agenda centered on reversing healthcare spending cuts and reducing household costs. In Michigan, Senate Republican nominee Mike Rogers publicly broke with the Trump administration over its trade war with Canada, a notable fissure heading into a tight race against progressive Democrat Abdul El-Sayed. And as Israel marks the third anniversary of the October 7 attacks, pro-Palestinian demonstrators disrupted a vigil in Manhattan, while in the Middle East, Houthi militants struck two airports in Saudi Arabia — including the capital Riyadh — killing three and wounding dozens more.

In baseball, the postseason rolls on. The Los Angeles Dodgers advanced to a third consecutive National League Championship Series after dispatching the Atlanta Braves, while the Tampa Bay Rays swept the New York Yankees in the American League Division Series, ending New York's season. The San Diego Padres, meanwhile, were outplayed by the Milwaukee Brewers in their division series, a quiet exit that San Diego fans are processing this morning.

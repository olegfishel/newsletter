Morning Briefing

Saturday, November 14, 2026

Sources: WSJ front page and The 10-Point, NYT front page and The Morning, San Diego Union-Tribune front page (A1 image) and e-Edition email, plus limited web reporting. Web searches returned little same-day coverage, so the wire context is thin.

Good morning, it's Saturday, November 14, 2026. The story dominating every front page is the fallout from the Nvidia spy scandal. Jensen Huang is out as chief executive of the world's most valuable chipmaker, and Washington is now arguing over who let it happen.

The Journal reports that interim CEO Colette Kress had questioned some of Huang's dealmaking before his ouster, and that he privately belittled her, calling her a deer in the headlights. The Times describes his exit as an abrupt resignation. It reports that President Trump phoned Huang on Tuesday and urged him to fight for his job, continuing a pattern of support even as the scandal intensified. U.S. officials fear Beijing's spies ensnared one of the biggest names in the AI boom. The Journal's 10-Point newsletter cites a European intelligence estimate that China has around 600,000 people working on intelligence and security. Its opinion page argues the scandal is China's, not America's. Inside the administration, blame is shifting. The Journal says some top Trump advisers are pointing at Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, who is fighting for his job over his inaction on chip export controls. The Times says Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is faulting Commerce for failing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore firm at the center of the affair. The Times adds that Huang's company holds roughly 48 billion dollars in stakes in hundreds of startups, so the Valley's uncertainty runs deep. It also reports that Asian Americans fear a backlash of hyper-scrutiny, and that Asia loses an AI ambassador to Washington. A separate Times piece asks why Silicon Valley turned on Anthropic, and notes that Anthropic's finances may get murkier when it goes public.

Closely tied to that is the White House shake-up. Stephen Miller is a week into his job as chief of staff. The Journal says he has run marathon meetings with cabinet secretaries and is preparing for a fight with Democrats, who plan dozens of investigations once they take control of Congress. The Times says Democrats also want strict new limits on White House budget powers. Hakeem Jeffries is locking down support to become speaker, and his allies say he deserves a unanimous vote. Senator Chris Van Hollen says he will challenge Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader.

The election aftermath produced more news. Democrat Scott Colom toppled the Republican incumbent in Mississippi's Senate race and becomes the first Black person elected to the Senate from the state since Reconstruction. In Colorado, MAGA firebrand Lauren Boebert lost her re-election bid. The Journal says she had clashed with Trump, and the Times calls the margin narrow. Thomas Massie is vowing more tough votes for Republicans in his final days. The Times says Biden now calls his 2024 debate performance not very consequential. The House passed competing veterans bills in a fight over funding.

The Supreme Court said it will review the Trump administration's policy of categorically denying asylum to people who cross the southern border. The Union-Tribune's front page, using New York Times reporting, says the policy rests on a proclamation declaring an invasion at the border. A decision is expected to land by the end of the court's term next summer. The paper's other border story says illegal crossings have dropped to the lowest in decades. Even so, Miller wants more, and is pressing for hard-line policies toward Mexico. The Journal separately reports that an international health body has revoked the U.S. measles elimination status it earned in 2000. The administration also said states may delay full Medicaid work requirements until July.

On travel, a nationwide FAA groundstop halted departures for more than four hours Friday evening. The Times says it was only the second full ground stop since 9/11 and that some 6,600 flights were canceled. The Union-Tribune says it was the longest in 25 years, and that San Diego International sat with planes stuck on the taxiway. Officials blame overloaded, antiquated air-traffic telecommunications that buckled when new technology was added. The Homeland Security secretary says there's no sign of a cyberattack but won't rule one out. The Journal and the Union-Tribune both warn of cancellations spilling into the weekend, and the Union-Tribune notes new disruption is possible. Microsoft had a rough stretch too. A global outage that the Journal says lasted 19 hours, and that disrupted governments, schools and businesses, was restored by early Saturday. Some email went undelivered.

Overseas, the Journal says Trump will meet Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China for an unprecedented three-way summit. An earlier wire report put it at an Asia-Pacific gathering in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19. On Ukraine, the Times says Russia has revived its demand that NATO pull back to its 1997 positions as part of a settlement. Europe has agreed on a team to represent it in talks with Russia, which the Union-Tribune also notes. Elsewhere, the Pentagon says Iran launched two drones at Bahrain and U.S. forces shot both down without injury. The Times says Trump is signaling a waiting game on Iran. A Times piece says Xi's military purges have elevated a new inquisitor. A U.S. Army officer accused of trying to spy for Russia is an Afghan war veteran. A Mexican prosecutor who helped the CIA operate secretly in his state has fled to the U.S. The pope's visit to Peru is set against growing competition to Catholicism. Democratic senators told Elon Musk to preserve records on SpaceX's limits on Ukraine's Starlink use. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot, who is trying to form the next government, accuses Netanyahu of stoking a racist campaign against Arab citizens. The U.N. chief is calling for drastic emissions cuts. Back in the U.S., a tanker crashed into a New Orleans riverboat with 129 aboard and partly sank it. The Times says everyone is accounted for. In Brooklyn, prosecutors opened a criminal inquiry into a building collapse that killed eight people, including two children.

On markets, the Journal says stocks were mixed Friday as rate fears resurfaced and the Nvidia turmoil dragged on the chip sector. The Dow gained 0.1 percent, the S&P 500 fell 0.4 and the Nasdaq fell 0.9. Nvidia closed the week down 13 percent, though the broader semiconductor index still gained 8 percent for the week. The Times calls it another volatile week in which stocks pushed ahead. It also says fewer Fed meetings raise the stakes for Warsh's communications.

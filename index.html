Morning Briefing

Wednesday, November 4, 2026

Compiled from The Wall Street Journal front page and 10-Point, The New York Times Morning newsletter, the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, and current web reporting.

Good morning, it's Wednesday, November 4, 2026. Voters delivered a sweeping rebuke to President Trump last night, and Democrats have taken control of both the House and the Senate. The New York Times calls it the blue wave Democrats hoped for and Republicans feared. Democrats have already won 228 House seats, with a larger majority expected as counting continues, and the Times estimates the party is on track to win the national House vote by about thirteen points, which would be the widest margin for either party in fifty years.

In the Senate, Democrats knew they needed to flip four seats, and they cleared that bar by evening as ordinarily safe Republican seats in Texas and South Carolina fell. James Talarico won in Texas, pediatrician Annie Andrews beat Senator Darline Graham in South Carolina, Susan Collins lost her Maine seat of three decades to Troy Jackson, and Sherrod Brown is returning to the Senate from Ohio. Democrats also flipped seats in Iowa and Kansas, and Abdul El-Sayed won in Michigan. Mississippi is too close to call, with the Democrat ahead by just a few votes, and Alaska is still counting. The governors' races produced a string of firsts, with Gina Hinojosa ousting Greg Abbott in Texas, Amy Acton defeating Vivek Ramaswamy in Ohio, Keisha Lance Bottoms winning in Georgia, and here at home, Xavier Becerra winning the race to succeed Gavin Newsom in California.

The Times points to heavy turnout, with Texas casting roughly ten million votes, a record for a midterm there, and counties dominated by white working-class voters swinging about fourteen points toward Democrats. A national voter survey for the Journal found an electorate deeply troubled by Mr. Trump, inflation, and the direction of the country, with high fuel and food prices a top concern. Republican efforts to redraw House maps largely failed to deliver, and a big spending advantage did not save them.

Mr. Trump acknowledged to the Journal late last night that the Iran war was partly to blame for his party's losses. Iran, he said, was a piece of it, though he added that he does not regret launching the war. He told the Times he is willing to accept the results and work with Democrats, while stressing that he still holds the veto pen. For Democrats, the win brings real leverage over nominees and government spending and the power to open investigations, but their promised action on healthcare and household costs will run straight into that veto.

The war itself, meanwhile, grinds on. American forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday, which officials described as defensive after Iranian forces fired on a U.S. aircraft. Qeshm sits at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz, and oil prices swung sharply, with Brent crude pushing above one hundred and five dollars before easing. The Journal reports that Tehran is using bomb-laden undersea drones, rocket-propelled grenades, and limpet mines against the vessels that shuttle oil out to waiting tankers, a shadow war inside the larger conflict that is aimed at pressuring Washington while negotiations remain deadlocked. Separately, Mr. Trump said the United States plans to cut off military aid to Israel by the end of the year, a timeline that suggests the phaseout of grants could happen far faster than the two governments had discussed.

In Ukraine, a Russian airstrike killed at least thirty-one people at a school in the south where humanitarian aid was being distributed, and nearly fifty more are missing. NATO, for its part, is developing a response doctrine that includes strikes far inside Russia should Moscow attack alliance territory. And North Korea accelerated a run of missile tests, signaling it will stick to its demands while spurning American diplomatic overtures.

On Wall Street, stocks finished Tuesday mixed as investors waited on the election. Futures dropped sharply overnight when the scale of the Democratic sweep became clear, with Nasdaq futures down as much as two percent, but they rebounded by early morning. S&P 500 futures are up roughly one percent and Dow futures about one and a half percent, as investors appear to be betting that gridlock will keep major policy changes off the table. The ten-year Treasury yield is hovering just under five percent. AMD jumped after its sales beat expectations, and the activist firm Elliott Management warned that today's markets look a lot like the Roaring Twenties that came before the Great Depression.

In corporate news, CVS Health will split into two publicly traded companies in the first half of 2028, separating its retail pharmacies from its insurance and pharmacy-benefit operations and reversing one of its signature deals. 3M agreed to pay about seven point three five billion dollars over nine years to settle claims over firefighting foam containing PFAS, the so-called forever chemicals. Shopify fell nearly nine percent after quarterly results that were not quite strong enough. China told its biggest securities firms and banks to slow their IPO work and focus on supporting the struggling stock market, and U.S. job openings rose to seven and a half million in September. Today brings the ADP employment report and the ISM services index, along with earnings from Uber, McDonald's, Kraft Heinz, DoorDash, Snap, Qualcomm, CVS, and Novo Nordisk.

A few other stories to watch. Investigators found pieces of the tail of the Bahamas-bound Learjet that crashed in Florida miles from the impact site, which suggests part of the aircraft broke away before it hit the ground. The Supreme Court appeared likely to side with Catholic preschools in Colorado that want public funding without enrolling children of LGBTQ parents. And hackers are reportedly cranking up the temperature in Europe's supermarket freezers to well above one hundred twenty degrees.

Closer to home, the Union-Tribune leads with the blue wave and Becerra's win in the governor's race. San Diego County's sales tax measure was narrowly split in early returns, a House contest has von Wilpert in the lead, a billionaire tax measure is trailing, and Elliott leads Bruce-Lane for state Senate. Locally, a group has launched a recall effort against Mayor Gloria, a heat wave and coastal flooding may arrive at the same time, and the Aztecs opened their season with a win over the Titans.

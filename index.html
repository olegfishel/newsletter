Morning Briefing

Thursday, November 5, 2026

Sources: The Wall Street Journal front page and 10-Point newsletter, The New York Times Morning newsletter, the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, and supplemental wire and web reporting.

Good morning, it's Thursday, November 5, 2026.

The country woke up to a very different Washington. Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate on Tuesday, along with a majority of the nation's governorships, in what the Wall Street Journal calls one of the most decisive repudiations of an American president in memory. Two close races, in Alaska and Mississippi, where Democratic challengers hold narrow leads, have still not been called. The Journal's county-by-county analysis shows how broad the swing was: of roughly three thousand counties with at least half the expected House vote counted, all but about a hundred and seventy ran behind Donald Trump's 2024 showing, and the median county moved about eleven points toward the Democrats. The New York Times adds that Democrats flipped at least five governorships and won full unified control of state government in at least five new states, cutting deep into territory once considered purple or even red.

The reasons, according to both papers, start with the war in Iran. The conflict sent gas prices up about forty percent and left many of the president's most loyal supporters disillusioned, and the Journal says Mr. Trump also lost his edge on immigration. Voters of every demographic group, the Times reports, swung back toward Democrats. The president himself conceded as much, acknowledging that Iran was partly to blame, though he told Fox he has no regrets about launching the war. He also said he accepts the results and expects to get along with Democrats, unless, in his words, they want to be destructive. Vice President J.D. Vance went further, publicly blaming the war for the Republican losses, a striking break with a campaign his boss still supports.

So what comes next? Chuck Schumer, the likely Senate majority leader, says his first job is bringing down the cost of living. Democrats want to reverse Medicaid cuts, restore Affordable Care Act subsidies, limit the president's power to impose tariffs, and curtail the war. But the president keeps his veto pen for the next two years, and he has already promised to be Mr. Veto if necessary. Expect oversight instead: Democrats are promising investigations into crypto windfalls, well-timed stock trades, and a wedding party for the president's son reportedly paid for by a Kremlin-connected oligarch. The Times notes the victory was more a rebuke of Mr. Trump than an endorsement of any one agenda, and Democrats remain split on impeachment, Israel, health care, and how much to tax the rich. One more footnote: Justice Samuel Alito has reportedly told people close to him that he does not plan to retire right away, which may have closed the window for another Trump Supreme Court appointment.

The Iran war itself may be nearing a turning point. Qatar-mediated talks have moved to Doha, and Iran's foreign minister says a plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz over two days is under discussion. President Masoud Pezeshkian said the supreme leader has granted the government authority to pursue peace talks. The two sides remain far apart, with Tehran demanding an end to the American naval blockade of its ports and relief on oil sanctions. Still, markets are betting on a deal. Oil had its biggest one-day drop since July earlier this week, and Brent crude is trading around ninety-three to ninety-four dollars a barrel this morning, with American crude near eighty-seven or eighty-eight.

Wall Street, meanwhile, cheered the result. The Dow jumped about fifteen hundred points on Wednesday, three percent, to close near fifty-three thousand, its best day after a midterm election in forty-four years. The S&P 500 rose about one and a half percent and the Nasdaq a bit less. The Journal describes a potential regime change in markets: investors are wagering on legislative gridlock, a slowdown in artificial intelligence spending, and checks on presidential power adding up to a more predictable economy. The ten-year Treasury yield slipped to about four point nine percent, and gold climbed back above four thousand dollars. Not every sector shared the joy, but makers of hip and knee implants stood out, with Zimmer Biomet up more than thirteen percent on hopes for Medicare-related procedures. This morning, Dow futures are higher while Nasdaq futures are lower. There is also a tech angle: voters backed candidates and ballot measures aimed at banning or slowing data centers, which could rein in A.I.'s rapid expansion.

In corporate news, CVS is planning to split its $400 billion healthcare empire, putting Aetna and the Caremark pharmacy-benefit manager in one company and its roughly nine thousand drugstores in another, a plan hatched in secret by a handful of executives. AstraZeneca offered about ten billion dollars for Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, Novo Nordisk raised its yearly forecast, and McDonald's reported higher profit as international strength offset soft American sales. Uber's growth slowed, BMW cut its guidance again, Volvo's global sales fell sixteen percent, and Google said it will invest fifteen billion dollars in Malaysia. China, for its part, blocked SoftBank's five-point-four billion dollar purchase of ABB's robotics unit.

Abroad, the Journal reports that Israel has ordered billions of dollars of American weaponry beyond what Washington has committed to pay for, which would make ending U.S. military aid hard. Israel would need roughly ten billion dollars after 2028 to cover those orders, even as the president says he plans to end the aid. North Korea has accelerated its missile tests while spurning American diplomatic overtures. Russian strikes disrupted power in Kyiv overnight. France is bracing for a nationwide general strike over living costs. In Hong Kong, the democracy activist Joshua Wong was sentenced to ten years in prison.

On today's calendar, weekly jobless claims offer a last look at the labor market before Friday's October jobs report, and the Bank of England announces its rate decision. Airbnb, Lyft, Cloudflare, and Carlyle report earnings. In sports, Cristiano Ronaldo announced he is retiring from the Portuguese national team. Closer to home, San Diego is still counting. Several local races, including the La Mesa mayor's contest and two in San Marcos, remain too close to call, and San Diego Unified's school bond measure is leading. And to end on a lighter note, the Journal tells of the children at a small lake in Hanoi who hunt American tourists, not for money, but for free English lessons.

WSJ What's News

Business & Finance

Stocks surged after American voters dealt a resounding blow to key pillars of Trump's economic agenda, with the S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq rising 1.6 percent, 3 percent and 1.4 percent, respectively.

AstraZeneca offered roughly $10 billion to acquire Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, a deal that would expand its portfolio of rare-disease treatments.

Pharmaceutical company leaders scorned the AI industry's optimism about the technology's capacity for drug discovery, saying the complexities of the search defy the hype.

Novo Nordisk raised its guidance for the year after noting an improved outlook for its key obesity and diabetes drugs, particularly in markets outside the U.S.

McDonald's reported higher quarterly profit and revenue as solid performance across much of its international business offset sluggish U.S. sales.

Uber's revenue growth slowed further in the latest quarter on weakness in its core ride-hailing business.

BMW again cut its annual guidance, warning that its core automotive segment could slide to a loss based on a closely watched profitability metric.

Volvo said its global sales fell 16 percent from a year earlier in the three months through October, hit by weaker demand in the U.S. and China.

Alphabet's Google will invest $15 billion in Malaysia to expand infrastructure, develop local talent and support clean-energy and clean-water initiatives.

World-Wide

Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress, a majority of governorships and more, delivering a sweeping rebuke to Trump and Republicans by uniting around a message of affordability.

The president will have a veto pen for the next two years, limiting Democrats' ability to enact laws that reverse his policies and setting up potentially heated clashes.

Vice President J.D. Vance vented his frustrations, blaming the Iran war for the GOP's losses and publicly repudiating the military campaign his boss still supports.

Americans' votes for candidates and ballot measures that ban or slow data-center development could rein in artificial intelligence's rapid growth.

New York Attorney General Letitia James stepped back from the criminal investigation into the alleged 2024 sexual assault at Cornell University.

A state appellate court ruled that the rollout of New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's pied-à-terre tax was legal and can proceed.

North Korea accelerated a series of missile tests, spurning U.S. diplomatic overtures with a growing list of demands.

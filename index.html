Good morning, it's Wednesday, August 5, 2026.

The dominant story at this hour is the high-stakes confrontation over the Strait of Hormuz, where the Trump administration and Tehran are locked in a volatile diplomatic standoff that has put global oil supplies — and the prospect of a major U.S. military strike — on a razor's edge. President Trump told reporters Tuesday that he was giving Iran "every last chance before decapitation," demanding a deal be reached by the end of the day. No agreement had been announced by morning. The two sides have been circling each other for weeks since a memorandum of understanding reached in June fell apart within a month. Trump called Iranian leadership "unbelievably duplicitous" after Tehran publicly denied that talks had resumed; Iran, in turn, called his statements a psychological operation. Oman is now serving as mediator, and Iran is proposing a single temporary corridor through the Strait — with Tehran remaining dominant — lasting one to three months. The Wall Street Journal describes Tehran's gamble in stark terms: the country is staking not just its economy but its relationships with regional neighbors on the bet that Trump will ultimately prefer a messy stalemate over all-out war. Eighteen American troops have been killed and nearly 700 wounded since the conflict began in late February.

Paradoxically, the mere prospect of a Hormuz deal ignited a powerful rally on Wall Street Tuesday. All three major indexes closed at record highs: the S&P 500 gained 1.8 percent to finish above 7,700 for the first time ever, the Nasdaq surged 2.6 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped more than 900 points. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent amplified hopes by striking an optimistic tone on the Iran negotiations. The breadth of the advance was striking — nearly 80 percent of Nasdaq volume came from advancing stocks. Semiconductor shares led the charge, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF jumping more than six percent. Palantir Technologies rocketed 29 percent. Futures pointed modestly higher again this morning, with investors now waiting on earnings from Disney and Shopify before today's open.

SpaceX reported its first quarterly earnings as a publicly traded company Tuesday, and the results were both impressive and unsettling. Revenue surged 92 percent year-over-year to $7.81 billion, comfortably beating estimates, driven by $4.3 billion from Starlink connectivity and $2.6 billion from the AI division that includes xAI and Grok. Elon Musk told investors the company's internal target for reaching $1 trillion in annual revenue had been moved forward a full year to 2030, with "a non-zero chance" of hitting it in 2029. He outlined plans to launch AI data centers into space by 2027, powered exclusively by Nvidia chips, with compute capacity expected to reach 10 gigawatts by end of next year. Despite the revenue beat, shares fell more than five percent after the release and continued sliding through the call, as investors focused on the net loss of $541 million and the sheer scale of capital expenditure required. SpaceX's stock closed Monday at $114.53 — already down 15 percent from its June IPO price and nearly 50 percent from the post-IPO peak, erasing more than a trillion dollars in market capitalization from that high.

In Michigan, the Democratic Senate primary produced results that were still being tabulated early this morning, though NBC News projected Abdul El-Sayed the winner over Representative Haley Stevens in one of the most watched contests of the midterm cycle. El-Sayed, a progressive physician and former state health director, held roughly 49 percent to Stevens' 47 percent with most precincts reporting; the Associated Press had not yet made an official call, with Wayne County ballots still outstanding. AIPAC spent more than $30 million backing Stevens, and El-Sayed's apparent victory is being described as a significant blow to the lobbying group. The race was framed as a referendum on the Democratic Party's direction, with the progressive wing on one side and the moderate establishment on the other. El-Sayed now faces former Republican Congressman Mike Rogers in November in a contest considered pivotal to control of the Senate.

On other political and domestic fronts: a divided Senate Judiciary Committee advanced Todd Blanche's nomination for attorney general after he committed in writing to eliminating a $1.8 billion fund that had rankled key Republicans. An armed man was arrested Sunday after he was found observing security operations at President Trump's golf course near Los Angeles, days before the president was scheduled to visit. The Trump administration also issued new Medicaid guidelines tightening oversight of autism therapy providers, a sector that federal investigators have been scrutinizing. And a Spokane resident was arrested on first-degree arson charges in connection with one of three major wildfires burning in eastern Washington, where thousands of acres have already been scorched.

In corporate news, Procter & Gamble announced it would acquire supplement maker Thorne for $3.8 billion, extending its reach into the health and wellness market. OpenAI agreed to pay $3.2 million to settle Justice Department claims that it discriminated against American workers by favoring temporary visa holders. McDonald's named a new U.S. chief to revitalize domestic sales, while Paramount reported another quarter of streaming growth offset by continued declines in its legacy TV business. Lufthansa shares fell more than eight percent in Frankfurt after the airline posted a steep drop in second-quarter profit and warned on its full-year outlook, a reminder that European carriers remain exposed to volatile energy and travel demand. Saudi Aramco, meanwhile, reported bumper profits through the first months of the Iran war despite significant operational disruptions — a signal of how elevated oil prices are shaping the geopolitics as much as the finances.

Here in San Diego, an extreme heat warning expired Tuesday evening after days of temperatures between 100 and 110 degrees Fahrenheit across much of the region. At the trade deadline, the Padres picked up veteran left-hander Robbie Ray to bolster a rotation that has been battered by injuries.

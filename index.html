Good morning, it's Monday, November 9, 2026. The dominant story this morning is a strange collision of corporate power, romance and espionage at the center of the artificial intelligence boom. Nvidia's board says it has opened an independent investigation into reports that its chief executive, Jensen Huang, was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. On Sunday, Huang told employees in an email that he had an affair with a businesswoman whom U.S. intelligence agencies believe was an asset of China's Ministry of State Security. She is reported to be linked to Megaspeed, a cloud company and Nvidia customer accused of helping restricted Chinese firms obtain advanced chips.

Huang has apologized, and the board says it remains confident in its co-founder. The inquiry is being run by independent directors with outside counsel. China's foreign ministry called the spy narrative unsubstantiated. Nvidia shares were down nearly five percent before the opening bell, and Congress is reportedly preparing a bipartisan look of its own. The timing is awkward. Huang has spent months lobbying Washington and other capitals to let chips flow freely, arguing that doing so does not threaten national security. The Times reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned senior administration officials about the relationship, and the Journal notes that members of Huang's family work at the company, which only deepens the question of how much power one executive holds over a four-trillion-dollar enterprise.

In Washington, the morning after the midterms is turning into the first full week of a new political era. Democrats are poised to control both chambers of Congress, though a couple of Senate races are still being counted. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom leads Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith by roughly twenty-seven hundred votes, and in Alaska, Mary Peltola holds a lead of just a few hundred over Senator Dan Sullivan with about three-quarters of the ballots tallied. President Trump, in his first sit-down interview since the losses, told "60 Minutes" that his immigration policies were probably too tough and may have hurt Republicans. He floated a deal with Democrats on immigration, hinted he might not endorse a successor in 2028, and said he has no plans to forgive his political enemies. Today Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation campaign, begins work as White House chief of staff, a sign the president intends to stay combative. Trump also says he was only kidding when he offered the press secretary job to UFC chief Dana White.

The results are also reshaping the road ahead. Vice President JD Vance and his allies are weighing whether a 2028 run still makes sense, with some worried that any Republican would struggle two years from now. Democrats gain the power to block Trump allies from the Federal Reserve board, the strongest check yet on his effort to control the central bank. Incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries, who has joined growing calls to remove Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, faces the harder job of uniting a divided caucus around affordability, ending the Iran war and limiting Trump's final two years. The Journal cautions that voters' economic demands are not easily met, since taxes and spending are slow tools and Trump still holds a veto. Congress returns today for a lame-duck session, with government funding set to run out on December 11.

The Times adds a striking detail about the incoming class. At least seven ordained Democratic clergy members will serve in Congress, more than triple the current number. Candidates like Texas Senate winner James Talarico and Kansas megachurch pastor Adam Hamilton wrapped progressive policies in scripture, and Michigan's Abdul El-Sayed will become the first Muslim senator. Whether voters were responding to faith or simply to frustration with Trump is nearly impossible to untangle, but it gives Democrats one possible template for 2028.

Overseas, diplomacy and war are moving at the same time. The United States, Russia and Ukraine are set to resume peace talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday, after the effort was suspended in February when the Iran war began. The sides remain far apart on territory, and Russia keeps hitting Ukrainian cities. In the Gulf, Yemen's Houthis struck an iconic tower in Riyadh, and Syria is reportedly preparing to send about six hundred special forces troops to help Saudi Arabia along its border with Yemen. In Israel, President Isaac Herzog has given Gadi Eisenkot the first chance to form a government. Climate diplomats gather in Turkey for COP31 without the United States, while scientists say the world is on track for nearly three degrees Celsius of warming. G-7 foreign ministers meet in Paris. And Poland is reeling from a mass shooting in Krakow that killed nine people, according to officials, with prosecutors treating it as terrorism.

On Wall Street, stock futures point to a strong open, up roughly one percent, with Dow futures near fifty-two thousand and S&P futures around seventy-four hundred seventy. Oil is down almost four percent to about eighty-seven dollars a barrel, the ten-year Treasury yield sits near four point eight five percent, gold is above forty-one hundred dollars an ounce, and Bitcoin is around seventy-four thousand seven hundred. A brighter-than-expected jobs report, helped by hiring in consulting, law and accounting, has eased immediate fears of a white-collar jobs collapse. Berkshire Hathaway's quarterly profit fell twenty-eight percent. Firefly Aerospace jumped thirty-seven percent Friday on a 1.3-billion-dollar engine deal with AST SpaceMobile. Citadel Securities and Cboe are suing over approval of single-stock perpetual futures, and crypto services have kept processing money tied to the six-billion-dollar Binance theft despite FBI warnings.

One last thought for the morning. At least twenty-three percent of Americans aged eighteen to thirty-four ran a business or side hustle last year, up from nineteen percent in 2021, as AI disrupts entry-level jobs and pushes young workers toward entrepreneurship. Nineteen-year-old Blades Brown became the youngest winner of a PGA Tour event in ninety-five years. And in Peru, authorities seized three thousand tarantulas hidden in socks at the Lima airport.

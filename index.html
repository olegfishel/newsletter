Good morning, it's Sunday, August 2, 2026. President Trump called off a new wave of airstrikes against Iran overnight, saying regional allies have reached the outlines of a deal that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz and wind down the five-month-old war — though no agreement has been signed and significant gaps remain between Tehran and Washington.

The framework Trump described would require Iran to immediately and completely reopen the Hormuz Strait and address its nuclear program, in exchange for an end to the American naval blockade and the right to resume oil exports. Israel has signaled it would back the arrangement, and mediation teams from Egypt, Qatar, and Turkey are pressing to lock in the details. The announcement stunned officials in both capitals, coming just days after Trump had ordered fresh strikes that military planners said could begin as soon as this weekend. NPR and Bloomberg both reported Saturday that no firm deal is in hand, only the outlines of one, and skeptics on both sides warn that the distance between an outline and a binding agreement can be vast.

The prolonged strait closure has already transformed the energy economy. ExxonMobil and Chevron both reported blockbuster second-quarter results Friday: Exxon earned roughly $14.5 billion — about $160 million a day — while Chevron posted net income of $12 billion, nearly four times what it earned in the same period last year and the highest quarterly profit in the company's history. British oil giant BP, by contrast, announced it is putting its U.K. North Sea business up for sale, a move that would end oil production in its home country after more than half a century. For consumers, the pain is spreading: Sherwin-Williams and the brewer of Samuel Adams are among companies raising prices on paint, beer, french fries, and more to offset higher commodity and freight costs tied directly to the war. The S&P 500 fell 0.1 percent for July and the Nasdaq lost 3.2 percent, though the Dow finished the month in positive territory.

On the Gaza front, the picture remained murky Sunday morning. A day after Trump heralded what he called a historic breakthrough toward the disarmament of Hamas, the militant group made clear it will not implement any part of the agreement unless Israeli forces first fulfill their own withdrawal obligations — and Israel has not publicly committed to the deal's terms. Israeli airstrikes continued in Gaza on Saturday, killing at least two people and damaging warehouses holding medical supplies. Separately, the Times published a visual investigation this morning showing that a U.S. strike destroyed a family home on Iran's Qeshm Island with a two-thousand-pound bomb, killing a husband, wife, and their two-year-old child — adding political pressure on the administration at a delicate diplomatic moment.

In the technology world, one of the year's defining business stories involves who now holds the artificial-intelligence crown. A major Wall Street Journal investigation concludes that OpenAI, once the field's unquestioned leader, lost ground by over-investing in consumer chatbots and flashy side projects, missing the critical coding-tools opportunity that rival Anthropic exploited to capture the industry lead. Meanwhile, Silicon Valley startups are racing to build American open-weight AI models to challenge cheaper Chinese rivals like DeepSeek and Qwen, but many venture investors remain reluctant to fund them, turning what is partly a technology race into a funding battle with national-security stakes.

On the domestic political front, the New York Times reports this morning that Trump's approval ratings are at or near the lowest of his presidency, according to several recent polls. The president revived a proposal for a $1.8 billion fund to compensate his political supporters if the Senate fails to confirm Todd Blanche as attorney general — and publicly rebuked U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro after she dropped vandalism charges against the Olympian accused of damaging the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. Senator Mitch McConnell has not been seen publicly since mid-June. The 10-Point today leads with a riveting reconstruction of the search for Nancy Guthrie — mother of NBC anchor Savannah Guthrie — who was kidnapped from her Arizona home months ago; reporters found that bad calls, bad luck, and strained relations between county and federal agencies derailed the investigation in its earliest hours.

Here in San Diego, the Union-Tribune leads Sunday with a look at how the sharp drop in illegal border crossings is reshaping operations at county checkpoints. The paper also examines the water supply hacking story — the scope of cyberattacks on U.S. water systems has reportedly widened — and carries the grim national headline of at least three people killed in a shooting at an Idaho In-N-Out restaurant. On the sports pages, it is a pivotal weekend for the Padres as General Manager A.J. Preller approaches Monday's Major League Baseball trade deadline, described as his time to shine; the team won Saturday in "full battle mode." San Diego FC earned a draw with Minnesota on a late penalty goal by Dreyer, and SDSU's Aztecs opened training camp with a healthy quarterback Denegal leading the depth chart.

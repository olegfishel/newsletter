Good morning, it's Sunday, November 1, 2026. Americans are two days from the midterm elections, and the mood inside the Republican Party is turning anxious. More than fifty million people have already voted early, and the final polling points in one direction: Democrats lead the generic congressional ballot by roughly eight or nine points among likely voters, with one recent survey putting it at fifty-two to forty-four.

Democrats need a net gain of just three seats to retake the House, where Republicans hold a narrow 218-to-214 edge, and the Cook Political Report now has Democrats favored in about 220 districts. The Wall Street Journal reports that Republican candidates, even in deep-red areas, are scrambling to shore up their support, and an NPR analysis found around seventy House districts where both parties are spending six figures on advertising in the closing days. The Senate is tighter, with the outcome hinging on toss-up contests in places like Maine, Ohio and Texas. Voters tell pollsters that the cost of living and preserving democracy are their top concerns, and forty-four percent say their vote for Congress is mostly a vote against President Trump.

Hanging over the final stretch is the war with Iran, which began on February 28. The Journal offers its fullest accounting yet of the damage Iran inflicted on American forces. Satellite imagery shows harm at more than two dozen U.S. installations, with the Pentagon estimating at least five billion dollars in losses. An Associated Press analysis found that Iran hit air defenses and early-warning systems first, then went after aircraft hangars, warehouses and troop housing, with Kuwait absorbing the most attacks. On the diplomatic side, reports say American and Iranian negotiators met for a third day of talks in Doha, and that Mr. Trump has said he will not order strikes before Tuesday's vote, though he also said the United States still has one or two targets in Iran. Iran's army, for its part, warned that any renewed war could last longer than the last one, and Tehran has designated Britain's Royal Navy and Royal Air Force as terrorist organizations.

On Wall Street, the artificial intelligence trade took a beating. A selloff in AI stocks snowballed all week and culminated Friday with the Nasdaq sliding nearly three percent, while the S&P 500 lost 1.8 percent and the Dow gave up 1.1 percent. Intel plunged about twenty-nine percent after its earnings, its worst day since 1974, and weak results from Amazon added to the gloom. Alphabet had tumbled more than twelve percent on Thursday as investors fixated on a simple question: who pays for all this spending, and when does it pay back? Alphabet's capital spending outran its operating cash flow last quarter. Apple has now passed Nvidia as the world's most valuable company at roughly five trillion dollars, and OpenAI is reported to have trimmed its funding target from thirty billion dollars to about twenty billion. The midterms and the next jobs report are next on the calendar for jittery investors.

In crypto, Binance is racing to cover losses from what the Journal calls the biggest heist in the industry's history, a hack of around six billion dollars that blew past the reserves the exchange had set aside. Binance has begun reopening withdrawals in phases, starting with bitcoin.

Elsewhere in business, the fuel-making divisions at ExxonMobil and Chevron posted their highest quarterly earnings ever. Ford's chief executive told workers the company expects to sell five million cars a year by early next decade. Universal Music Group shares leapt on signs of recovery in a closely watched streaming metric, and federal safety regulators are investigating Comma.ai after at least five crashes were linked to its thousand-dollar hands-free driving device. On health care, the Affordable Care Act marketplaces opened this morning for 2027 coverage, and premiums are set to rise thirteen percent, the second straight year of double-digit increases in a market already facing shrinking enrollment.

Abroad, Ukraine struck an oil refinery in Russia's Samara region, ending a two-week pause in refinery attacks that came amid pressure from the Trump administration. Canada and the European Union laid out an ambitious new partnership, forged in a matter of weeks out of their separate disputes with Mr. Trump. In Poland, the president held crisis talks after a dramatic showdown at the constitutional court over judicial independence. And in a legal development, a federal judge threw out Mr. Trump's ten-billion-dollar lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal and Rupert Murdoch over a story about his ties to Jeffrey Epstein.

Closer to home, a few San Diego items from local reporting: police made more than 160 arrests in a crackdown in the Gaslamp Quarter, the San Diego Zoo plans to keep charging for parking, and Colonel Charles Dudik has taken command at Marine Corps Air Station Miramar. And as a closing note from the Journal's front page, neighbors across the country are feuding over increasingly gruesome front-yard Halloween displays, with parents pushing back on scenes they say are terrorizing small children.

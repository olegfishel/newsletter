Good morning, it's Monday, September 28, 2026. The week opens with bond markets testing levels not seen since 2007, diplomacy in the Iran war at a volatile standstill, a freshly struck U.S.-China trade deal, and a major hurricane bearing down on northwestern Mexico.

The dominant story shaping financial markets right now is a striking breakdown in the economic theory that guided the Trump administration's early months. The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.21 percent on Friday — its highest level since 2007 — as investors confronted a sobering reality. Trump's advisers had argued that demonstrating fiscal seriousness would reassure bond markets and let long-term interest rates fall on their own, giving the president room to leave the Federal Reserve alone. It hasn't worked out that way. The Wall Street Journal reports this morning that rising deficits, the ongoing war with Iran, and the energy price shock that conflict has delivered have combined to push inflation higher and forced the Fed to keep raising rates rather than cutting them. Tariffs added to consumer prices, oil above ninety-four dollars a barrel has strained household budgets, and a corporate investment boom has kept the economy running hot. The Journal asked six leading money managers — including BlackRock's Rick Rieder and Ray Dalio — what investors should do. Their consensus: yields above five percent will eventually compress stock valuations, but the equity market has so far shown a surprising resilience compared with the punishment being dealt out in bonds. That tension between a punished bond market and a still-standing stock market is the defining financial subplot of this year, and this morning Wall Street futures point lower as the week begins.

On the diplomatic front, the Iran conflict remains at a dangerous impasse. President Trump over the weekend formally rejected Tehran's proposal for a seven-day truce that would have reopened the Strait of Hormuz and restarted nuclear negotiations. Iran had conditioned the pause on a halt to what it called American acts of aggression, an end to the naval blockade, and the release of frozen Iranian assets — terms Trump called unacceptable. He told aides he expects to resume bombing after November's midterm elections. Mediators are now pressing Iran to make a concrete concession on its nuclear program in hopes of reviving broader ceasefire talks; the Wall Street Journal describes it as a long-shot bid. Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi told reporters Sunday that his country was still awaiting an "official" American response to its latest proposal. Adding to the tension, British authorities announced the arrest of five men on suspicion of plotting a terrorist act near an air base in Gloucestershire that the United States has been using in the Iran conflict — a reminder that the war's risks extend well beyond the Middle East. Closer to home, a San Diego-based aircraft carrier has been deployed to the region, according to the Union-Tribune.

There is notable movement on U.S.-China relations after President Xi Jinping's three-day Washington summit last week. Beijing and Washington agreed to a thirty-billion-dollar reciprocal tariff reduction — covering American agricultural goods and Chinese small appliances, toys, and decorations — and launched a formal dialogue on artificial intelligence, a striking development given the fierce technology rivalry between the two powers. The summit also produced commitments to attend each other's APEC and G-20 hosting events. The diplomatic thaw comes even as a controversy simmers: U.S. Ambassador to China David Perdue told an interviewer that Trump at one point offered to sell American arms to Beijing during Xi's visit, though White House officials quickly said there were no plans for any such sale. Two giant pandas that Xi promised during the summit have already arrived in Atlanta.

In technology and artificial intelligence, the Journal takes a deep look this morning at the doomer philosophy rooted in effective altruism that has shaped Anthropic — and its fears about AI wiping out humanity. The story notes the significance of President Trump's recent dinner at the White House with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, a pivotal moment for a company that has been a target of administration criticism. Meanwhile, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg used his company's annual developer conference to promote a planned fusion of the Muse AI agent with the company's smartglasses, pushing the vision of an always-on AI companion into the mainstream.

Boeing disclosed a software glitch in the 737 MAX that could cause an automated navigation feature to fail during landing — yet another chapter in the aircraft's troubled safety history. The company says it is working on a pilot procedure and a software fix. Also in aviation, the San Diego Union-Tribune's discovery that a U.S. aircraft carrier has departed for the Middle East underscores the pace of operational deployments in the Iran conflict.

Weather is on the radar on both coasts. Hurricane Polo is bearing down on Baja California Sur in northwestern Mexico with maximum sustained winds of 115 miles per hour. On the East Coast, a rare September nor'easter swept from New Jersey to New England over the weekend, cutting power to more than 100,000 customers and killing at least one person — a New York City Housing Authority worker struck by a falling tree in rain-soaked Manhattan.

The New York Times leads its Morning newsletter with a striking nutrition story: Americans spent roughly ninety billion dollars in the past year on food products marketed for their protein content — including protein vodka, protein Pop-Tarts, and protein Kraft Mac and Cheese. The government's latest dietary guidelines, updated with MAHA-coded language, increased recommended protein levels by more than 50 percent. Food companies are happy to oblige, even as scientists warn that excessive protein consumption can contribute to heart disease and kidney problems. Meanwhile, a Times investigation finds that the Trump administration is brokering deals to mine gold in Venezuela and export it, with local gangs taking a cut of the profits.

In sports, American cyclist Brandon McNulty won the Road World Championships in Quebec on Sunday, becoming the first American to claim the title in 33 years. The Los Angeles Chargers dropped to oh-and-three on the season after an ugly loss to the Buffalo Bills. The National League Wild Card Series begins this week. The U.S. Presidents Cup golf team claimed an eleventh consecutive title. And at Madison Square Garden, the WNBA playoffs are underway, with New York, Golden State, Atlanta, and Las Vegas all winning in the opening round.

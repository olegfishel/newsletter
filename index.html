Morning Briefing

Wednesday, August 26, 2026

Sources: Wall Street Journal (front-page capture & 10-Point newsletter), The New York Times Morning newsletter, San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, supplemented by Reuters, NPR, Bloomberg, and Washington Post reporting.

Good morning, it's Wednesday, August 26, 2026. The country is waking up to the death of a beloved American icon, a diplomatic surprise in Moscow, and a trade war with our nearest neighbor that is growing uglier by the hour.

We begin with Dolly Parton, who died Tuesday at the age of eighty after a brief battle with cancer. She passed at Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center in Nashville, surrounded by family. Her nephew Brian Seaver announced the news on her social media. For nearly six decades, Parton was the rare entertainer who seemed to belong to everyone — she held the top rank on country radio from 1967 to 1993, accumulated twenty-five number-one singles, and earned entry into both the country and rock-and-roll halls of fame. She was also a philanthropist, a wit, and an irresistible cultural force. "I've created this and played it up — the makeup, the whole persona," she said in 2002. "But this isn't all I am." Paul McCartney, Taylor Swift, Beyoncé, and Barack Obama were among those who offered tributes. "It is hard to imagine living in a world without Dolly," McCartney wrote. She was eighty years old.

The other story dominating this morning is the escalating trade war between the United States and Canada. Ottawa announced it will impose retaliatory tariffs of fifteen, twenty-five, and fifty percent on roughly seven hundred American products worth about twenty billion dollars — a direct dollar-for-dollar answer to the levies President Trump imposed after trade negotiations broke down last Friday. The new duties will take effect September eighth and target steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, electronics, and more. Prime Minister Mark Carney, who has declared his country "at war" with the United States economically, said the decision "will raise costs and reduce choice for Canadians" but that there was no other choice. The Journal notes that Canada is now "punching and counterpunching with a swagger" rarely seen from Ottawa. Trump, for his part, threatened Tuesday to rename Lake Ontario to Lake America as the confrontation grew more personal. The Journal's What's News section added that congressional Republicans — usually deferential to Trump — have begun to push back, particularly over his move to lift restrictions on importing ground beef, which they say will hurt American ranchers ahead of midterm elections.

In one of the more startling diplomatic developments in months, CIA Director John Ratcliffe made an unannounced eight-and-a-half-hour visit to Moscow on Tuesday — the first publicly known trip by a Trump administration official at that level to the Russian capital. Ratcliffe traveled via Latvia on a U.S. Air Force C-17. The CIA declined to comment on the purpose of the meeting, but reports indicate he met with Russian intelligence counterparts. Ahead of the visit, Washington asked Kyiv to suspend strikes until the delegation had departed. The trip comes as the conflict in Ukraine enters its fifth year with no clear resolution in sight.

On the financial markets, all eyes today are on Nvidia, which reports second-quarter earnings after the close this afternoon. Analysts are forecasting revenue of about ninety-two billion dollars — essentially double last year's figure — making it a critical test of whether the AI buildout that has powered markets for two years is still on solid ground. The Journal's 10-Point notes that the report lands as regulators are separately investigating Situational Awareness, the AI-focused hedge fund run by twenty-something wunderkind Leopold Aschenbrenner. The fund managed forty-five billion dollars at its peak before losing billions and selling off most of its portfolio in July; the SEC is now examining whether its trading was above board. The story is a cautionary tale about the intersection of Silicon Valley fervor and Wall Street ambition.

The Wall Street Journal's front page also highlights the unfolding diplomatic squeeze on Iran. The United States announced new sanctions targeting Iran's gold, digital assets, aviation, shipping, and tech sectors — but notably stopped short of penalizing China, which has been Tehran's primary economic lifeline throughout years of American pressure. Separately, a British maritime authority reported an oil tanker was struck and disabled near the Strait of Hormuz, further straining the standoff in the region. Trump submitted a landmark nuclear accord with Saudi Arabia to Congress for review, though he has not dropped his last-minute condition requiring Riyadh to normalize relations with Israel before the deal can proceed.

In domestic news, Senator Darline Graham — who inherited the South Carolina Senate seat from her brother, the late Lindsey Graham — won her Republican primary runoff Tuesday, demonstrating that Trump's endorsement still carries significant weight in GOP primaries even as his national poll numbers soften. The Times's coverage this morning catalogues a growing list of defiance: European leaders have declined to join the U.S. campaign against Iran, Israel's prime minister rejected Trump's Gaza plan outright, and bond markets continue to resist his fiscal policies. The Times notes that none of this should lead anyone to underestimate Trump; his ability to move markets with a single post and his continued influence over the Republican primary electorate remain formidable.

Bill Gates offered a stark warning this week, saying artificial intelligence companies were racing ahead with the technology without any coherent plan for the economic disruption that would follow. And in a public health note, two unvaccinated Pennsylvanians have died from measles this year — the worst year for the disease in the United States since it was declared eliminated in 2000. Finally, SpaceX announced plans for a new hundred-billion-dollar launch facility in southern Louisiana, its second private site after Starbase in Texas.

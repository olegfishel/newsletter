Morning Briefing

Friday, October 16, 2026

Compiled from the Wall Street Journal front page and 10-Point newsletter, The New York Times Morning newsletter, the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, and current wire and web reporting.

Good morning, it's Friday, October 16, 2026. We begin in Oregon, where a community is mourning after a shooting at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend. Police say a sixteen-year-old blasted his way through locked doors on Thursday and killed three children, ages five, eight and eleven, along with a teacher. An armed bystander followed the gunman into the building and shot him dead. The attack came the same week The New York Times and the San Antonio Express-News published an investigation into the other side of school security in Texas, where districts have spent billions putting police on campuses since the Uvalde massacre. The reporting found that about forty-eight hundred children between ten and thirteen were referred for prosecution last school year, a third more than a decade ago, often for things like a playground scuffle or vaping in a bathroom.

Eighteen days from the midterms, the political temperature keeps climbing. The Wall Street Journal's Washington coverage chief, Damian Paletta, says there are growing signs, even among some Republicans, that the House will flip to Democratic control. The Senate is a different story. Democrats need a net gain of four seats, they feel good about North Carolina, and Republicans think they might steal a seat in Michigan. His advice is to watch the candidates rather than the polls, and to notice who looks desperate and who looks confident. Republicans are spending hundreds of millions of dollars to defend an expanding map, and President Trump is barnstorming through states like Montana and Nebraska. Meanwhile the Justice Department has sued California to stop it from counting mail ballots from twenty thousand people the administration says may not be citizens, and the FBI is investigating suspicious mailings, including letters said to contain suspicious substances, sent to election offices and senators across the country. Election officials say they are increasingly worried that the president's attacks on the integrity of the vote could set up a challenge to the results.

Overseas, the Journal has an inside account of how Washington's posture toward Ukraine turned colder. Limits on Ukraine's access to military-grade Starlink terminals set off panic among Kyiv's officials and several American lawmakers. At a European Union summit in Brussels today, President Zelensky said Ukraine is dependent on Starlink to intercept Russian jet-powered drones, and that he sees no sign Moscow is ready to end the war. European leaders rallied around him, while Mr. Trump called Ukraine's renewed strikes on Russian refineries a disgrace. That came days after he announced an energy-strike halt that Mr. Zelensky says Russia never agreed to. Overnight, Russian attacks left at least six hundred thousand people without power around Kyiv and Kharkiv, and the two sides have begun hitting each other's data centers. The Journal's newsletter also tells the story of a twenty-six-year-old Russian lieutenant, Lev Stupnikov, who fed Ukraine his army's plans and helped it kill more than three hundred Russian soldiers before slipping across the front line.

In the Middle East, the Iran-backed Houthis struck the presidential palace in Aden, Yemen's interim capital, killing at least four people. Saudi Arabia accuses Tehran of encouraging the escalation, and the Saudi-led coalition says Houthi forces have attacked Riyadh's airport twenty times this month. Iran's army commander says the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until Iran's demands are met, though U.S. Central Command says commercial and energy traffic is still moving with American and partner support. A maritime monitor reports three strikes on ships in the waterway this week, and Brent crude is hovering around one hundred and five dollars a barrel.

On Wall Street, stocks rose on Thursday. The Dow gained 1.1 percent, the S&P 500 rose 0.3 percent and the Nasdaq edged up 0.1 percent. The ten-year Treasury yield settled at 5.086 percent, down from 5.189 on Wednesday, and futures are slightly higher this morning. Retail sales rose in September but more slowly than in August. Mortgage rates have pushed above seven percent, their highest in more than three years, which is making a tough housing market tougher. Fuel prices are rising across the country, and the Journal notes the pain is uneven in ways that could matter at the ballot box. On a brighter note for endowments, Harvard reported a twenty-seven percent return, lifting its fund to a record sixty-eight billion dollars, with gains from SpaceX and other AI bets helping universities across the country.

In business news, the Journal reports that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has objected to significant tariff cuts on Mexican steel, aluminum and cars, stalling progress toward a trade deal for the second time recently that he has upended talks with a North American partner. Netflix's chief content officer, Bela Bajaria, is leaving as the company struggles to spark growth, and Peter Rice will succeed her. Meta released an open-weight AI model and is working with Apple so it can run locally on high-end Macs. TSMC says it may add U.S. chip capacity beyond Arizona, KFC is switching to Coca-Cola as its beverage supplier at thousands of restaurants in a blow to Pepsi, and Ericsson shares surged on strong network sales. After nine years of delays and hype, the Tesla Roadster has finally launched.

A few other stories are worth your time. President Trump says the execution of Nidal Hasan, the Fort Hood shooter, will not be livestreamed, reversing a Pentagon plan. Prosecutors are accusing a Silicon Valley engineer of hiding a recorder in an OpenAI conference room. A year after the Louvre jewel theft, more than a dozen museums have been hit by smash-and-grab thieves, and the French crown jewels are still missing. Back on the lighter side, a small dog named Luna, lost in Yellowstone for nearly five weeks, was found safe, and a Brooklyn toy store is now asking fifteen million dollars for a toy bird that Kelly Clarkson says it refused to sell her.

Business & Finance

Major U.S. stock indexes rose, with the S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq gaining 0.3 percent, 1.1 percent and 0.1 percent, respectively, while the yield on the 10-year Treasury note settled at 5.086 percent, down from 5.189 percent on Wednesday.

Sales at U.S. retailers rose in September, but at a slower pace than in August, according to Commerce Department data.

Netflix's chief content officer, Bela Bajaria, is leaving the company as it struggles to engage viewers and stimulate growth, and she will be succeeded by Peter Rice.

Meta Platforms released an open-weight artificial-intelligence model and is collaborating with Apple to run it locally on the company's high-end Mac computers.

TSMC raised the prospect of additional U.S. manufacturing capacity beyond Arizona as demand for AI chips continues to outpace supply.

KFC struck a deal to take on Coca-Cola as its beverage provider at thousands of U.S. restaurants, a blow to longtime supplier PepsiCo.

YouTube has had discussions with publishers about developing an audiobook feature that could be ready next year.

Pernod Ricard said it is preparing for a potential listing in India, hoping to capitalize on growing demand there as sales fall in many of its other markets.

Ericsson shares surged as investors cheered strong growth in sales of network gear, shrugging off a warning that higher parts costs will weigh on profitability next year.

World-Wide

Progress toward a U.S.-Mexico tariff deal stalled after opposition from Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, the second time recently that he has had a hand in upending advanced negotiations with a North American partner.

The Justice Department sued California in an attempt to block the state from counting mail-in ballots it receives from 20,000 people the Trump administration says may not be U.S. citizens.

The FBI said it is investigating suspicious mailings sent to election offices across the country just weeks before the midterm elections.

Three children and one teacher were killed in a shooting at an elementary school in Bend, Oregon, and the 16-year-old suspect was shot and killed by a community member who followed him into the school.

Republicans are playing defense on an expanding electoral map, spending hundreds of millions of dollars in the final stretch to prevent a Democratic takeover of Congress.

Minnesota has become a political Rorschach test after two state flashpoints were nationalized and folded into this year's midterm debate.

President Trump said the firing-squad execution of condemned killer Nidal Hasan will not be livestreamed, reversing a decision by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Good morning, it's Thursday, November 12, 2026. The story dominating every front page today is Nvidia, where the future of founder and chief executive Jensen Huang is in serious doubt.

The Wall Street Journal reports exclusively that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Huang after a company investigation into two extramarital affairs, one with a woman U.S. intelligence has identified as a Chinese spy, and another with an executive who reports to him. The Journal adds that Huang was introduced to the first woman by her own husband, a Chinese businessman he had known for about a decade, who appeared to accept the relationship for two years and helped her pursue business ventures across China, Singapore and Taiwan. The Times is more cautious on where things stand: it says the board met to discuss Huang but has announced no decision, and that the shares plunged, while some of Silicon Valley rallies around him as essential to the A.I. race, given that Nvidia supplies roughly ninety percent of the chips behind the big A.I. projects. The Union-Tribune's wire story frames it as a board meeting to weigh the CEO's fate amid a second affair allegation. Web reporting from earlier this week is more restrained still: the board announced an independent investigation on November 8, said it had confidence in Huang, and three House committee chairmen asked him to attend a closed-door briefing on November 18. So the Journal's account is the most aggressive, and it may reflect newer reporting. The Times also runs a news analysis arguing that the episode shows a fierce spy war persisting beneath the friendly tone between Trump and Xi, and notes that Huang's wife, who has kept a notably private profile, is now in the spotlight. The Journal's Jonathan Weil adds that Nvidia's disappearing Singapore sales are deepening scrutiny of a company called Megaspeed, and a Journal profile follows a thirty-one-year-old trader who called out Huang's China problem and shorted the stock.

That drama lands as the A.I. trade shows strain. The Journal says TSMC approved an investment of up to a billion dollars in Anthropic and OpenAI, giving markets a lift yesterday, with the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow gaining 1.3, 1.6 and half a percent. This morning, though, Nasdaq futures are down about one percent and S&P futures slightly lower. The Times says Anthropic's giant IPO ties it more closely to Nvidia and will hand a big payday to the largest tech companies. The Journal also warns the global economy looks vulnerable to an A.I. bust, especially in Asian countries heavily exposed to the American data-center build-out, while the Times asks whether voter backlash against A.I. could actually help the economy.

In politics, Democrats are still absorbing their midterm wave. Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat in a historic upset after more than a week of counting, becoming the first Black senator from the state since Reconstruction. The Journal's newsletter says Democrat Annie Andrews took the South Carolina seat long held by Lindsey Graham, even carrying Greenville County, the spiritual home of the modern Republican Party, where a former chief of staff to the governor said his party's drubbing was deserved. Republicans have chosen Jim Jordan as House leader and John Thune as Senate leader, and the Times reports Bernie Sanders opposes Chuck Schumer as Democratic majority leader. Senate Republicans are also racing to confirm federal judges before they lose the chamber.

Democrats have their own embarrassment. Eleven House Democrats missed the vote on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, enough to have changed the result, and the effort failed, angering activists, though some blamed traffic. Hegseth drew more attention on Veterans Day when a soldier scolded his group for talking and laughing near the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. Trump visited Arlington as opposition grows to his proposed arch, and Democrats are targeting his proposed new class of battleships. A coalition of Democrats and a few House Republicans also forced a new vote later this year on Epstein files.

The White House has warned the incoming Democratic chair of the House Oversight Committee that aggressive investigations could amount to a crime. The Times says it is unclear what legal basis supports that, since the Constitution gives lawmakers immunity for legislative acts, and the Union-Tribune carried the Times story on its front page. Meanwhile a top Democrat vows oversight of the CIA's covert actions. The Times also says Trump hinted at a bipartisan immigration deal and then reversed himself, as he has before. And in an odd note, a California man is about to win an election though he died months ago.

Tuesday, October 13, 2026

Compiled from The Wall Street Journal (front page and The 10-Point), The New York Times (The Morning), the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, and supplemental web reporting. The WSJ front-page text capture timed out, so its What's News items were read from the front-page screenshot.

Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 13, 2026. The dominant story is the widening shadow of the Iran war, which now stretches from the Persian Gulf to the English countryside. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham told the House of Commons on Monday that Iran's Revolutionary Guard was almost certainly behind a suspected plot against RAF Fairford, the base in southwest England that American bombers used in strikes on Iran. Five men were arrested near the base on September 27, and police found gasoline in three vans, though no explosives. Tehran denies any role and says Britain should produce its evidence. The Journal's front page adds that Iran is fortifying its nuclear sites and rallying loyalists, and is threatening a wider and harsher response against the United States and its Gulf allies if it is attacked again after the midterm elections. The ceasefire that held from April into July collapsed when fighting resumed on July eighth, and Tehran appears to be preparing for a second round.

The Gulf is also under pressure from Yemen. A Houthi missile struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday, killing twelve people, including an American, and wounding more than three hundred. It was the deadliest attack on the kingdom in over a decade, and the Houthis have kept firing, with another ballistic missile aimed at the capital's airport. President Trump says he is weighing whether the United States should join the fight against the Houthis. A Journal opinion piece notes that the whole region is watching what Washington does when the cornerstone of its Gulf alliance system comes under direct attack, and a front-page story says Iran-backed militants are tightening the screws on Saudi Arabia's crown prince.

In Europe, President Trump announced on Sunday an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine. President Zelensky said the news was new to him, and Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure continued that same day. It follows Trump's claim after a Friday call with Vladimir Putin that Moscow will supply more than four million tons of diesel to world markets. European officials are criticizing the arrangement, some lawmakers say it clashes with American sanctions, and the Journal reports it likely won't do much to ease fuel prices before next month's midterms. NATO's top military commander, meanwhile, laid out a new plan to thwart a potential Russian assault along the alliance's eastern flank, and the Baltic states remain on alert.

At home, the midterms are only weeks away and the political news is crowded. Federal agents searched Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar's Washington home on Monday as part of an inquiry into possible fraud in her family's immigration paperwork, which she denies. The Justice Department has long avoided investigative steps this close to an election. Trump said he is looking at firing Kimberly Guilfoyle, his ambassador to Greece, after reports that she asked a donor to cover personal expenses before her confirmation hearing. A filing shows ten megadonors supplied more than half of the three hundred million dollars Trump raised for the new White House ballroom and related projects. In Texas, the Journal reports that Senate nominee Ken Paxton celebrated New Year's Eve 2024 in Dubai with two fugitives from American justice, one of whom had given him a hundred and ninety thousand dollars. Democrat James Talarico holds a narrow lead, and he is back on the trail after more than a week away with the flu, a stretch in which Republicans spread a false rumor about him.

On the markets, Monday ended mixed, with the Dow up about four-tenths of a percent and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq slightly lower; the bond market was closed for Columbus Day. This morning, Dow futures are up about six-tenths of a percent, S&P futures are flat, and Nasdaq futures are down about half a percent. In Seoul, the KOSPI plunged more than six percent and fell below 6,000 on a sharp selloff in chipmakers, with SK Hynix down as much as nine percent and Samsung nearly as much. Oil settled near ninety-four dollars a barrel after the energy-ceasefire announcement. Earnings arrive from JPMorgan Chase, Citigroup, Wells Fargo and Goldman Sachs, September existing-home sales are due, and Apple is expected to unveil new smart-home products. In deals, dating app Bumble is in advanced talks to go private at a valuation near a billion dollars, and quantum-computing company IonQ agreed to buy the cybersecurity firm Elisity for up to 1.3 billion dollars.

Elsewhere, the Journal's lead feature looks at China's Gen Z, who grew up in boom years and now face a weak economy, rapid adoption of artificial intelligence and a record crop of graduates, leaving many burned out and betrayed. The Paris Motor Show is hosting about twenty Chinese brands, up from nine two years ago. Two Americans, Robert Hall and Michael Woodford, won the Nobel Prize in economics for their work on how household and business expectations underpin economic stability.

For a lighter note, commuters into Manhattan from New Jersey face five weeks of bus, ferry and train disruption starting today as workers upgrade a bridge. NASA's Perseverance rover has set a distance record on Mars at 28.1 miles. The Journal also tracks down the story of Bill Jones, the Navy catapult operator Trump says inspired his order to bring back steam catapults on aircraft carriers, though the Navy has never been able to find him. In baseball, the Brewers beat the Dodgers seven to four to take a two-games-to-none lead, and the Guardians won Game 1 over the Rays.

And in San Diego, the Union-Tribune reports that the fight against wildfires is turning to artificial intelligence and hundreds of cameras, while El Niño drives a record Pacific storm season and more big waves and high tides are expected along the coast. In sports, the Chargers face pressure to turn things around after a blowout, with the quarterback spot the hot topic, and the Aztecs host Fresno State.

Business & Finance

Major U.S. stock indexes ended mixed on Monday, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq losing 0.06 percent and 0.4 percent, respectively, and the Dow gaining 0.4 percent, while the U.S. bond market was closed for the Columbus Day holiday.

Lawsuits are piling up against Dodgers owner Mark Walter, brought by senior citizens who purchased annuities from his insurance companies.

A bitcoin-mining business that appeared left for dead after the collapse of Sam Bankman-Fried's crypto empire is being resuscitated to serve the artificial-intelligence build-out.

Meta Platforms released a new AI model whose usefulness will be judged by how much it can improve retention and speed adoption of the company's Muse agent.

Quantum-computing company IonQ struck an all-stock deal to buy the closely held cybersecurity firm Elisity in a transaction valuing it at up to $1.3 billion.

Dating-app company Bumble is in advanced talks to be taken private in a deal valuing it at roughly $1 billion, a report that sent its stock sharply higher even though shares remain down about 95 percent from their post-IPO peak.

Frenzied stock-options trading shows that individual investors are still hungry for risk.

The developer of the $10 billion One Beverly Hills project is asking condo buyers to lend it money through so-called Founder Bonds that pay 5 percent, a rarely seen financing tool that some buyers say leaves them carrying extra risk.

Morning Briefing

Monday, November 16, 2026

Sources: WSJ front page and The 10-Point, NYT front page and The Morning, San Diego Union-Tribune front page (A1 image) and e-Edition email, plus limited web reporting. Web searches returned few results dated today, so cross-checking beyond the three papers was thin; the Nvidia filing detail comes from an SEC 8-K.

Good morning, it's Monday, November 16, 2026.

The story that runs through all three papers this morning is the fall of Jensen Huang. Nvidia's founder stepped down as chief executive and board member last week, and a company filing says CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim chief, with Harvey Jones named the board's first chair. The Times front page tells the story behind it: Huang, it says, carried a secret for more than a year, a relationship with a female customer that he had described as purely business, and that concealment is what ultimately cost him the job he created. The Union-Tribune's front page carries the Associated Press angle, which is narrower: Singapore data-center operator Megaspeed says the woman at the center of the espionage allegations, Alice Huang, left the company in August 2025, and that it has opened an outside-led internal investigation into rumors about Nvidia chips reaching Chinese buyers. The Journal, meanwhile, leads with two Huang pieces of its own. One traces how huge Southeast Asian data centers gave Chinese companies computing power and, allegedly, cover for chip smuggling. The other describes a campaign by his allies, through open letters, phone calls and group chats, arguing his ouster was hasty, even as legal scrutiny grows. The Times business section adds that Tech leaders are rallying behind him and that Wall Street has lost its biggest prize, and it reports that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick pushed to weaken chip rules that overlap with his family's business. The scandal has raised bipartisan pressure on President Trump to tighten chip export controls just as he heads to China this week for a meeting with Xi Jinping, and the Times says chief executives are also gathering there. The Journal's newsletter adds that Nvidia will be named one of more than two dozen AI "national champions" in an administration effort to help American AI firms win business overseas.

That feeds straight into markets, where the Journal says the Nvidia shock, the election's aftermath and a punishing run since the Nasdaq's October 6 peak have ended the easy era of AI investing, so investors must now pick winners and losers. Futures are mixed this morning: Dow futures are roughly flat, the S&P 500 futures are down a few tenths of a percent, and Nasdaq futures are down about one percent. Crude oil is near 88 dollars a barrel, gold is around 3,870, and the 10-year Treasury yield is above 5 percent. One more AI item: Anthropic's long-awaited public offering is slated for this week and is expected to value the company at 1.33 trillion dollars or more, with more than 300 investors on its books, according to the Journal; the Times says Wall Street is bracing for the company's aggressive spending. The Journal also notes that November's Democratic wave has complicated tech companies' data-center plans, since opposition to data centers is a rare point of agreement among Democrats.

In the Middle East, Iran attacked Bahrain for the second time in three days. Bahrain's defense ministry said its forces intercepted several Iranian aerial threats on Sunday, and the Revolutionary Guard also made claims about the strike, which came two days after the U.S. military shot down Iranian drones. Negotiations to end the war remain at an impasse. The Journal reports that U.S. intelligence figures Iran still has between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles, which counters President Trump's claims and weighs on discussions about whether to resume strikes. The Times adds that an emboldened Iran is feeding anxiety in Europe, and that Saudi-backed forces have retreated in Yemen, leaving a key strait under Houthi control. Elsewhere, the Times says relentless Russian strikes on Ukraine's power grid threaten to make some cities unlivable, and the Union-Tribune notes Ukrainian President Zelenskyy saying Starshield limits are deterring drone use. Serbia's student movement, which beat the country's strongman at the polls, now faces the question of whether it can govern, and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney is touring Asia to build trade ties. Europe is seeing protests the Times attributes to a generational clash, and one of France's most popular athletes is running for president. Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo, the Mexican drug lord who laid the foundations of the modern drug trade, died at 80. The pope is seeking justice for slain forest defenders, per the Union-Tribune. And the Times reports that at least 5,000 people have died since May in an Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo, which experts fear could surpass the 2014 to 2016 West African epidemic.

At home, the other big story is Friday's nationwide air-travel shutdown. The Journal's exclusive pins it on a single router in southern Arkansas, with one in Texas also involved, where a data overload on an antiquated FAA network stalled trips for hundreds of thousands of travelers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, who made the call to halt flights, describes it as starting with routine technical upgrades at an Arkansas radio facility, and he is now arguing for more aviation money. The Union-Tribune's A1 says the administration is pressing Congress for billions to modernize the system, while Democrats demand to know why billions already spent haven't fixed it, and Mr. Trump has weighed in on social media. The Times reports delays and cancellations persisted as the system recovered, and that two planes collided on a taxiway at Miami International. A Journal opinion piece says money alone won't fix a system that needs a new governing model. The Times' news analysis ties it to the week's Microsoft Outlook outage, which Microsoft says is fully restored, as a lesson in our dependence on fragile technology.

In politics, Democrats are celebrating midterm victories while arguing over who deserves the credit. The Morning says both moderates, who flipped red-state seats, and progressives, such as Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan, credit the big-tent approach, though Times analyst Nate Cohn finds the left-wing candidates ran behind their moderate peers. The Times says Senator Chris Van Hollen reports growing support for a Senate challenge to Chuck Schumer, and that Democrats are already looking toward 2028. The Journal reports Democrats are torn over whether to float new taxes to pay for a multitrillion-dollar agenda, including expanded healthcare. The Times also notes that conceding an election is no longer a given, and that Black women in Georgia are taking seven of the top eight elected leadership roles in the state. The Journal reports that the U.S. attorney in Houston is threatening to investigate congressional Democrats who investigate the administration, and that Stephen Miller, newly named White House chief of staff, has formed an unlikely alliance with Marco Rubio. Other items from the Journal: the administration will let states delay Medicaid work requirements by six months, a change expected to cost millions their coverage; a federal judge blocked the closing of a Washington public golf course for a championship-level makeover; the White House, after consulting two Trump allies, ordered the release of tens of millions of dollars in detained illegal Chinese vapes; and California's Proposition 42, banning new state taxes on personal property, is set to become law. Rahm Emanuel is betting the country wants a radical centrist, according to the Journal's Greg Ip.

In business, Tesla is taking orders for a personally owned Cybercab, with no steering wheel or pedals, at around 29,990 dollars. BMW has launched a ChatGPT app in the U.S., and ChatGPT now lets users pay through an AI agent. Verizon's marketing chief is remaking its outreach with social-media-first messaging. Family Dollar will pay 63 million dollars to settle overcharging claims, the FDA rejected a closely watched Chinese lung cancer drug, and auto troubles are hammering German towns. The Journal reports a job market split in two, with white-collar seekers searching longer than in years, and says a Long Island mall, Samanea, rebounded by becoming an entertainment destination. Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday, with Baidu and Tyson Foods among today's reports.

Other national items. The Times covers the rescue of a toddler from the rubble of a fatal Brooklyn building collapse, and reports the developer avoided stricter oversight beforehand. New York police fatally shot a driver who officers said was ramming cars. The Times also reports on a sorority influencer, a crash of New York's specialized high school admissions test, deed theft, and Trump's plans to remake D.C. International students are avoiding American universities for a second year. A meteor shower peaks tonight, though the bright moon will make it hard to see. The Journal's lighter pieces: a backyard battle over Amazon delivery drones, and a lawyer who has traveled the globe without boarding a plane since 1997.

Closer to home, the Union-Tribune's front page leads with Mission Bay's long-monopolized boat storage slots on the sand bars. The city's program is being revamped after an audit found vague rules, low fees and poor record keeping, with hundreds of people on a waiting list for about 270 slots. The plan would use a lottery, higher fees and clearer rules, and goes to a City Council committee Tuesday. The paper also reports custody time in local ICE activity has nearly doubled, with more transfers, according to a new report on the Otay Mesa Detention Center, and the county votes on expanding its legal defense program for immigrants. Also in San Diego: families who lost loved ones are gathering, a local chef was named a Robb Report "Power Player," three San Diego burritos ranked among the nation's best, Encinitas will debate raises for council members, and residents got some answers on a plan update.

In sports, the Chargers visit the Ravens, with the Union-Tribune noting an old friend in Minter, and the paper highlights a razzle-dazzle fake field goal and a "grit over glamour" piece. Boise State hosts the Aztecs, who then host Texas Southern in basketball; Torresani is an unlikely catalyst. Nationally, the Seahawks beat the Raiders 21-13 in Fernando Mendoza's debut. Toronto won the WNBA draft lottery, and Kansas City and Washington meet in the NWSL final. The U.S. and Canada play for women's hockey world gold.

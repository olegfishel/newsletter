Morning Briefing

Thursday, November 5, 2026

Sources: The Wall Street Journal (front page and The 10-Point), The New York Times (The Morning), San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, with supplemental reporting from Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, CBS News and NBC News.

Good morning, it's Thursday, November 5, 2026. We begin the morning after a political earthquake. Democrats have won control of both the House and the Senate, along with a majority of the nation's governorships, in an election the Wall Street Journal calls one of the most decisive repudiations of an American president in memory.

The Journal's editor in chief, Emma Tucker, puts the blame squarely on the war in Iran, which sent gas prices up roughly forty percent and left many of the president's most loyal supporters disillusioned. Mr. Trump also lost his long-standing advantage on immigration. Democrats, long divided, unified around a single message of affordability, and voters rewarded it. The Journal's county-by-county analysis found that among about three thousand counties with at least half the House vote counted, all but roughly one hundred seventy ran behind Mr. Trump's 2024 margin, with the median county swinging about eleven points toward the Democrats. The Times' Nate Cohn writes that there is no modern precedent for the tsunami, and voters of every demographic group moved left.

In the House, Democrats are on track for somewhere between two hundred fifty-five and two hundred fifty-eight seats, a level they have not reached since 1993. The Senate majority was clinched when Josh Turek defeated Republican Ashley Hinson in Iowa, though two races, in Alaska and Mississippi, where Democratic challengers hold narrow leads, have yet to be called. At the state level, Democrats flipped at least five governorships and won full control of government in at least five new states.

The recriminations have already begun. Mr. Trump has acknowledged that the Iran war played a part in the defeat. Vice President JD Vance went further, blaming the war for the losses and publicly breaking with a military campaign his boss still supports. The president retains his veto pen for the next two years, and has promised to be what he calls Mr. Veto if necessary, but his tone since Tuesday has been conciliatory. He said he will probably get along with Democrats unless they want to be destructive, and floated cooperation on the debt ceiling and immigration enforcement. Chuck Schumer, the likely Senate majority leader, says job one is lowering costs. Democrats want to reverse Medicaid cuts, restore Affordable Care Act subsidies, limit the president's tariff powers and curtail the war. They also promise aggressive oversight, from crypto windfalls to well-timed stock trades to a wedding party for the president's son reportedly paid for by a Kremlin-connected oligarch. The Times cautions that the result was more a rebuke of Mr. Trump than an endorsement of any agenda, and Democrats remain split on impeachment, Israel, health care and taxes.

The war itself has not paused. On the eve of the election, American forces struck Iranian targets around the Strait of Hormuz, including the islands of Qeshm and Hengam and the area near Bandar Abbas, targeting forces that had been firing surface-to-air missiles at U.S. aircraft, according to a U.S. official. Iran says it would reopen the strait within two days if Washington meets a set of demands, but a senior Iranian official told Reuters the two sides remain far from a deal. And a related story from the Journal: Mr. Trump said Tuesday he plans to end aid to Israel, yet Israel has ordered billions of dollars in American weapons beyond what the U.S. has committed to fund, and would need about ten billion dollars after 2028 to pay for outstanding orders, according to a State Department document sent to Congress.

Wall Street, for its part, loved the result. On Wednesday the Dow jumped more than fifteen hundred points, or about three percent, its best day after a midterm election in forty-four years, while the S&P 500 rose 1.6 percent and the Nasdaq 1.4. Oil fell more than eight percent, with U.S. crude settling near eighty-eight dollars a barrel. The Journal says the blue wave has set off a potential regime change in markets, as investors bet on gridlock, a more predictable economy and checks on presidential power. This morning, Dow futures are up about seven tenths of a percent, S&P futures are little changed, and Nasdaq futures are down nearly a percent, as the technology sector digests another message from voters. Candidates and ballot measures to ban or slow data-center construction did well across the country, including in Ohio, and the Journal says that could rein in the rapid growth of artificial intelligence.

In corporate news, CVS is quietly planning to break itself up, a secret plan hatched by a handful of executives. Aetna and the Caremark benefits manager will form one company, and its roughly nine thousand drugstores another. AstraZeneca offered about ten billion dollars for Rhythm Pharmaceuticals, Novo Nordisk raised its full-year guidance, and McDonald's reported higher profit as international strength offset soft U.S. sales. Uber's growth slowed, BMW cut its outlook for the second time and warned its core auto business could slip into a loss on a key profitability measure, and Volvo's global sales fell sixteen percent. China blocked SoftBank's five-point-four-billion-dollar purchase of ABB's robotics unit, and Google said it will invest fifteen billion dollars in Malaysia.

Elsewhere, North Korea has accelerated its missile tests and is rebuffing American overtures with a growing list of demands. France is bracing for a nationwide general strike over the cost of living, after riots the Journal says were fueled by an off-the-grid ultra-left faction. Russian attacks have disrupted power in Kyiv, and Israel's president has begun talks to form a governing bloc. In Hong Kong, activist Joshua Wong was sentenced to ten years in prison. Back home, Justice Samuel Alito has told people close to him that he does not plan to retire right away, which may close Mr. Trump's window to name another justice. And in sports, Cristiano Ronaldo announced his retirement from the Portuguese national team, ending a career that spanned six World Cups.

Uber's revenue growth slowed further in the latest quarter on weakness in its core ride-hailing business.

BMW again cut its annual guidance, warning that its core automotive segment could slide to a loss on a closely watched profitability metric.

Volvo said its global sales fell 16 percent from a year earlier in the three months through October, hurt by weaker demand in the U.S. and China.

Alphabet's Google will invest $15 billion in Malaysia to expand infrastructure, develop local talent and support clean-energy and clean-water initiatives.

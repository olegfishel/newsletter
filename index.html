Good morning, it's Tuesday, November 24, 2026.

The story everywhere this morning is Nvidia. Eleven days after he resigned over revelations of two extramarital affairs, Jensen Huang has been reinstated as chief executive. The board brought him back unanimously after a weekend of pressure from the White House. The Times says President Trump personally assured directors that Huang would not face criminal charges and did not pose a national security risk, with Vice President Vance running much of the weekend maneuvering. The Journal calls it an unprecedented move by a president to bend a company board to his will. Trump told the Times he simply thought Huang should be running the company, and that he hadn't expected the effect on the stock. One of the two affairs was with a woman the Times and Journal both describe as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. Trump has dismissed that, telling CNBC that people call any good-looking Chinese woman a spy. Even so, the administration has barred a company she founded, Megaspeed, from buying advanced A.I. chips, and the Times separately reports Nvidia has been asked to sell chips to companies tied to the Chinese military and to Megaspeed.

Markets loved it. By the Journal's count the Dow rose 3 percent, the S&P 500 4.7 percent and the Nasdaq 6.5 percent, which put more than two trillion dollars back into the Nasdaq after last week's selloff. Nvidia's shares jumped 31 percent, adding nearly a trillion dollars in value; the Times and the Union-Tribune's Times wire version say simply "more than 30 percent." Dell rose about 20 percent in sympathy and reports earnings this afternoon, while Hewlett Packard Enterprise slipped after hours on results that fell short. This morning, though, futures are pointing lower, with the Dow off about half a percent and the Nasdaq down more than one and a half. The ten-year Treasury yield sits near 4.9 percent, the volatility index is elevated near 29, oil is around 80 dollars a barrel and gold is above 3,600.

The political backlash is bipartisan. Congressional Republicans sharply criticized the move; Senator John Kennedy said it appears the Commerce Department let China get around chip export controls, and Democrats, who take over the House next year, say more investigations are coming. Senator Ruben Gallego accused the president of selling out national security to keep the stock market high. Huang is due before four congressional committees next week, including a public Senate hearing. Trump added to the strangeness by posting an A.I.-generated meme that swaps a bloodied Huang for himself in the famous Butler, Pennsylvania photograph. The Times says its reporting helped break open the investigation, and its columnists and the Journal's opinion page both ask what this says about the president's priorities, with the Journal calling it the "Trump market put" that will carry legal and political costs. The San Diego Union-Tribune led its front page with the story, using the Times account.

Elsewhere in business, CrowdStrike agreed to buy cloud-security firm Zscaler for 43.9 billion dollars, which the Times calls a combination of two major cybersecurity firms at 44 billion. New research linking Roundup's main ingredient to cancer complicates Bayer's litigation just as a judge approved a 7.25 billion dollar settlement for most of the lawsuits. A Dutch court provisionally barred sales of BYD's large SUV in the European Union, finding it copies the design of the Land Rover Defender. Nestlé's chief executive says the backlash against ultraprocessed foods isn't fading. ConocoPhillips will sell its Norwegian oil and gas business and a stake in a U.K. oil terminal to Var Energi for about 3.9 billion dollars, and Texas Pacific Land plans to buy EagleRock Land in an all-stock deal valued near 3 billion. The Journal also tells the story of a former pharmacist whose 875 million dollar startup sale to McKesson went toxic, with founders fired and accusations of "buy-and-kill," and how investor Mark Walter helped Magic Johnson become a billionaire through an insurer now linked to a federal probe. Its personal-finance piece looks at adult children crushed by eldercare costs, and another feature follows a home-health aide in Pennsylvania. The Journal also reports that Amazon has hit setbacks building a satellite-internet rival to Starlink, and that Anthropic's newly minted multimillionaires are heating up San Francisco housing. The Conference Board releases November consumer confidence today, and Analog Devices, Medtronic, Autodesk and Workday report earnings. The Times adds that Oregon and Virginia are suing SpaceX over sexualized A.I. images produced by Grok, that CVS Health's planned breakup hasn't quieted antitrust critics, and that outage alerts and A.I.-uncovered software flaws point to more tech disruptions ahead. The Journal's number of the day is 17 million, the lines of code in a newly published proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, found by Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge working with the company's Claude model; two independent groups say they have full confidence in it.

Abroad, Iran and Israel are back in focus. The Journal reports the U.S. is investigating Iran's possible role in cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers, some with bomb threats, and that Russia is also a suspect, amid worries Tehran is widening the war. The Times analysis asks whether Prime Minister Netanyahu's push to renew strikes on Iran serves Israel or himself, with military analysts and opposition leaders leaning toward the latter. Israeli political leaders also denounced "Jewish terrorism" after attacks in the West Bank. In Ukraine, the Union-Tribune says Zelenskyy is hailing the sinking of a Russian warship, and the Times reports a Ukrainian naval-drone attack off the coast of Libya that shows the reach of its fleet. It is not clear from these papers whether they are the same incident. A Times analysis of hacked Russian data finds more than 700,000 Russians have died in the war, the Journal says testimony points to widespread sexual abuse of Ukrainian prisoners of war, and the Times describes Ukraine's older volunteer partisans and a psychological-warfare radio operation playing Russian rock on the front line. The Times also reports how Melania Trump built back channels to both sides of the war through her longtime agent. In Colombia, a drone attack hit a city and injured at least three people, with the U.N. reporting a 445 percent rise in drone bombings; the Union-Tribune covers it too. Mexico captured a major drug lord this weekend with U.S. drone and intelligence help, which the Journal says also shows the limits of Mexico's strategy, and the Times asks whether Mexico is truly stepping up. Crime bosses hiding in the U.A.E. may find it less safe after new treaties with Western authorities. The Times also reports fake pro-China personas targeting Taiwan's election, China's A.I. anxieties and a Chinese comedian whose shows are selling out after he mocked Xi Jinping. The U.N. climate talks broke down. Prince Harry and Meghan are in Britain, and Pope Francis's decades-long diary of doubts has come to light.

In Washington, besides the Nvidia fight, a House Ethics report found substantial evidence that Democratic Representative Jimmy Gomez of California committed sexual misconduct with a staffer, and he says he will resign. The Journal calls it misconduct; the Times says unwanted sexual advances. Redacted documents behind the search of Representative Ilhan Omar's home show prosecutors seeking evidence from decades back, from before she was born by the Journal's front-page account, and as far as 1980 in its newsletter. Companies tied to an investment firm affiliated with Donald Trump Jr. are a target for congressional Democrats. Trump condemned California Governor Newsom for commuting the death sentences of more than 500 inmates and says he will seek to speed federal executions. Celebrities and Democratic donors signed a letter backing Chris Van Hollen over Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader, the Democratic Party plans 12 primary debates for 2028 beginning in June, and the number two official at the F.B.I., Christopher Raia, will retire. The Journal says Republican transplants are leaving Texas for Oklahoma after the Democratic midterm wave, and the Times notes a debate over energy permitting in which Trump's pet projects are tangled up. Harvard has taught a for-credit course on the MAGA movement, and the Pentagon is talking to General Motors and other manufacturers about mass-producing drones.

Across the country, the Army will blow up about five tons of degraded explosives at an Iowa ammunition plant tomorrow, the day before Thanksgiving, after an appeals court cleared the way. The Times says the blast may spread lead dust and force some residents indoors for up to two days. In Chicago, a wealthy woman and her neighbors are feuding over rats, which locals blame on squirrel feeding, and both the Journal and Times have the story. Doctors warn measles is a growing risk for adults vaccinated before 1989, and the Union-Tribune carries that Times piece. Other Times stories include two New York police firings over racism, a Virginia school district closed for a week after abuse claims, a Cornell case, a collapsed building in an excavation investigation, a Section 8 ruling in New York, lab-made drugs sold at gas stations, hospice eligibility rules, and a California sewage problem during heavy rain. Thanksgiving dinner for ten costs about 58 dollars and 63 cents, up 6 percent. The culture pages cover the new Hunger Games prequel, Miley Cyrus's wedding, a Maldon salt controversy, restaurant phones returning, Tamagotchi nostalgia and Universal's Earl the Squirrel in the Macy's parade.

In San Diego, the Union-Tribune's front page features a coastal flood watch: an El Niño-enhanced king tide is expected to push well up local beaches Wednesday and Thursday, with a coastal flood watch in effect through Thursday afternoon. House Democrats from San Diego laid out priorities, with local immigration enforcement high on the list, and plan to press for oversight of the Trump administration's tactics. Home prices barely budged in San Diego County even as sales hit a 21-year low, with the median near 926,000 dollars; experts blame high mortgage rates and sellers who are not desperate. San Diego Gas and Electric changed the proposed route for its Powerlink transmission line to avoid Temecula, where officials had protested. The city is halting its brush crackdown in a fire-prevention pivot, the school district paid 2.4 million dollars to settle lawsuits, two Surf Cup lease suits were combined, and an Apple TV film was shot in town.

In sports, the Padres' new owners say they are all in for the community. San Diego State plays Iowa State in the Players Era tournament and then travels to Washington State. The Chargers lost to the Jets as a weak offensive line left quarterback Justin Herbert in jeopardy, and they host the Patriots next. Nationally, the Commanders beat the Bengals with a last-second comeback, the NWSL's Washington Spirit fired its coach after a third straight championship loss, and the S.E.C. will meet next week on whether to remove L.S.U.

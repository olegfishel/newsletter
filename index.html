Morning Briefing

Monday, November 9, 2026

Sources: The Wall Street Journal (front-page capture and The 10-Point), The New York Times (The Morning), and the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, supplemented by current wire and web reporting.

Good morning, it's Monday, November 9, 2026. The business world wakes up to an unusual mix of corporate scandal and national security, while Washington begins adjusting to the shape of a new political era.

The dominant story this morning is Nvidia. Chief executive Jensen Huang has apologized for an affair with an executive who is suspected of working for Chinese intelligence, and the company's board says it is investigating reports that he was the target of a foreign espionage effort, while expressing confidence in its co-founder. The New York Times reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned senior administration officials about the relationship, and it asks a pointed question: is the man who has become the maestro of the artificial intelligence boom too powerful to be fired? The timing is awkward. Huang has spent months lobbying Washington and capitals around the world to let advanced chips flow freely, arguing that doing so would not compromise national security. Nvidia shares were down roughly five percent in premarket trading, and the Journal adds a human wrinkle: Huang's children work at the company, and he has apologized to Nvidia for his infidelity.

In Washington, Congress returns today for a lame-duck session before both chambers pass into Democratic hands. In his first sit-down interview since the Republican drubbing, President Trump floated a deal with Democrats on immigration, acknowledged that his deportation policies were probably too tough, suggested he might not endorse a Republican successor in 2028, and said he has no intention of forgiving his political enemies. He has also elevated Stephen Miller, the architect of the mass deportation campaign, to White House chief of staff, starting today, a sign that the combative posture will continue. Hakeem Jeffries, the presumptive House speaker, has joined growing calls to remove Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and the Journal notes that Democrats will now have the power to block any allies Trump might try to install on the Federal Reserve board. Vice President JD Vance, meanwhile, is weighing whether a 2028 run still makes sense. A couple of Senate races remain unresolved: in Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola leads Senator Dan Sullivan by just 637 votes, and in Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom is ahead of Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,700. The Times adds a curious footnote: Democrats will more than triple the number of ordained clergy in Congress, with faith-infused campaigns built on love of neighbor that may offer the party a template for 2028.

Overseas, the war with Iran grinds on alongside tentative diplomacy. Trump says fresh strikes are highly unlikely if talks make progress and Tehran signs a strong agreement, but Rubio, on his way to Paris for a two-day meeting of G7 foreign ministers, said a breakthrough is not imminent and called Iran's demands unacceptable. Both sides report vessels hit near the Strait of Hormuz. Yemen's Houthis, who are fighting Saudi Arabia as another front in the wider war, fired missiles at Riyadh over the weekend; Saudi air defenses intercepted them, but falling debris blew out windows across several floors of the Aramco tower in the financial district, with no injuries reported. A drone attack also briefly halted production at Iraq's Khor Mor gasfield. Crude oil is down nearly four percent this morning, to roughly 87 dollars a barrel.

There is movement on another front. Russia, Ukraine and the United States are set to hold talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday, the first since February, with Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner leading the American side and security guarantees at the top of the agenda. The parties remain far apart on territory, and Russia continues to strike Ukrainian cities. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot has been given the first chance to form a government following the elections.

On Wall Street, futures are higher as dip-buyers step in after the S&P 500's worst week since April 2025. Dow futures are up about one percent, and S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures are each up nearly one percent. The ten-year Treasury yield sits near 4.85 percent, and gold has climbed above 4,100 dollars an ounce. Investors face an inflation report and another round of tech earnings this week. Berkshire Hathaway reported a 28 percent drop in quarterly net income as investment gains slumped. One bright spot was Firefly Aerospace, which jumped 37 percent Friday on a 1.3 billion dollar deal to supply rocket engines to AST SpaceMobile. On jobs, a stronger-than-expected report, helped by hiring in consulting, law and accounting, has eased immediate fears of a white-collar employment collapse, even as the Journal reports that more twenty-somethings are skipping entry-level jobs altogether. At least 23 percent of Americans aged 18 to 34 now run a business or side hustle.

A few other stories to note. The United Nations climate summit, COP31, opens this week in Turkey without the United States, and scientists say the world is on track for 2.8 degrees Celsius of warming, nearly double the 1.5-degree target. A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck Japan, injuring at least 19 people. A train collision in Egypt killed seven and injured 125. Poland is reeling after a rare mass shooting in Krakow that left nine people dead. In South Africa, the party of Nelson Mandela suffered another electoral rebuke. And Pope Leo's visit to Argentina is highlighting a growing rift between the Catholic Church and the libertarian president, Javier Milei.

Closer to home, the San Diego Union-Tribune reports on a smart wall of AI cameras watching the border and the people who cross it, and says the Metropolitan Transit System has already spent 700,000 dollars preparing for El Niño disruptions. Xavier Becerra will be the state's fifth Catholic governor in a row. San Diego marked its first Kumeyaay People's Day, and Santee voters are leaning against a one-cent tax hike. In sports, San Diego State started strong in a win over UTSA at Viejas Arena, and the Chargers meet the Ravens.

Good morning, it's Saturday, November 21, 2026. The story driving the weekend is a brutal week on Wall Street, centered on the stock market debut of Anthropic.

The Journal reports that Anthropic's shares fell 8.5 percent on their second day of trading Friday, erasing about 109 billion dollars of market value, and that investors are now arguing over who got shares and whether the opening price was set too high. The Journal also says Nasdaq stocks lost more than 4 trillion dollars this week, wiping out the index's gains for the year. The Times frames it as the stock market's worst week in more than a year, with the S&P 500 ending more than 10 percent below its recent high, and says tech stocks sank even with Anthropic's blockbuster offering, as investors grew wary of the A.I. boom. The Journal adds a profile of the siblings behind the company, Dario and Daniela Amodei. One web wrap-up of the debut day said the stock opened well above its offer price and then faded, and the Nasdaq fell more than 2 percent that day.

More A.I. and tech news. President Trump threatened to ban American tech companies from building data centers outside the United States, floating a tremendous tax or other penalties. The Times says the S.E.C. is investigating Nvidia's accounting practices, and that the woman at the center of the Nvidia spy scandal, Alice Huang, has extensive ties to China's defense industry. The Times also reports that an Anthropic mathematician claims, with help from A.I., to have proven the Riemann hypothesis, the most famous unsolved problem in mathematics. And the Journal's Saturday essay notes a run of outages, from Microsoft Outlook to air-traffic control grounding every departing U.S. flight, to banks, airlines and even the National Security Agency, asking whether the bigger worry is how we recover.

Overseas, Polish officials say three Russian objects, including at least one missile, crashed into Poland's territory, and the prime minister called it an intentional provocation. Denmark says it will expel Russia's defense attaché after accusing Moscow of ordering the attempted killing of a Ukrainian-American rapper on Danish soil. British and European leaders agreed to remove some of the barriers introduced by Brexit, a decade after the U.K. vote to leave. Mexico captured a drug kingpin who was among the F.B.I.'s ten most-wanted fugitives. In France, student protests over school conditions spiraled into riots, with the far right blaming immigrant neighborhoods, a far-left leader blaming police tactics and the government blaming ultraleft agitators, all while France's colorblind tradition bars the collection of race statistics. Elsewhere, the Journal shows how El Salvador's president, Nayib Bukele, is taking his gang-fighting strategy to Haiti, and notes that Canada's Mark Carney has visited 32 countries since taking office, spending 113 days abroad, more than twice as many as Trump over the same period. In Washington, the Times says the war with Iran is likely to be a fight for the new Democratic majority in Congress, which could pass legislation to end it or limit funding, though Trump could veto and Democrats lack the votes to override. The White House plans to scale back its use of special government employees, who keep private-sector pay or investments while serving. The Times reports exclusively that an internal watchdog found ICE went too far last year in loosening training standards to hire more officers, and that federal watchdog lawyers opened and then backed off an inquiry into possible fraud around Trump's memecoin. ICE agents also detained a Chinese-born Princeton neuroscientist as she traveled from a conference, and the number of new science Ph.D. students at major U.S. universities has fallen substantially.

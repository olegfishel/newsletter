Morning Briefing

Tuesday, October 20, 2026

Compiled from The Wall Street Journal (front page and The 10-Point), The New York Times (The Morning), the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition, and supplemental wire and web reporting.

Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 20, 2026. France is trying to regain control after a weekend of the worst rioting the country has seen in years. Protesters smashed shopfronts and set fire to the town hall in Limoges, and the Journal reports that bands of rioters have looted thousands of stores and raided city halls across the country. The unrest grew out of weeks of demonstrations over the education system and the cost of living. On Monday night, authorities imposed a curfew in two areas of Paris, around the Place de la République and the Châtelet-Les Halles district, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu says those measures helped keep the peace. More curfews will follow tonight where they are needed.

The crisis has become a political one as well. President Emmanuel Macron is facing criticism for leaving on a three-day tour of Gulf energy suppliers, meant to secure oil and diesel and bring down prices at home, just as the country burns. The beneficiary appears to be Marine Le Pen, the far-right frontrunner for next spring's presidential election, who is presenting herself as the one candidate willing to restore order. The Times notes that the anger in France reflects frustration across Europe over sluggish growth, high inflation, and dim prospects for young people.

That Gulf trip is no accident, because energy is the backdrop to almost everything this morning. Saudi Arabia has postponed its flagship investor conference, the so-called Davos in the Desert, which was due to open in Riyadh next week, and pushed it to 2027. The reason is a sustained barrage of missiles and drones from Iran-backed Houthi militants in Yemen. An attack on Riyadh's King Khalid airport earlier this month killed at least twelve people and wounded more than three hundred, and the Houthis claim fresh strikes on another airport, on Aramco facilities on the Red Sea coast, and on the Shaybah oilfield. Britain says it will expand its military support for Riyadh, including defensive air patrols. Crude oil is trading around one hundred and ten dollars a barrel, its highest level since May, and President Trump announced eight billion dollars in aid for farmers squeezed by high diesel prices.

Wall Street goes into this with a mixed mood. U.S. stocks ended Monday with the S&P 500 up four-tenths of a percent and the Nasdaq up more than one percent, while the Dow was essentially flat. This morning, futures are pointing slightly lower for the S&P and Nasdaq, and the ten-year Treasury yield sits above five percent. The most closely watched report today comes after the closing bell, when Netflix delivers third-quarter results. The Journal reports that the streaming giant is in the middle of a creative shake-up. Last week it replaced its content chief, Bela Bajaria, hours after a layoff of a thousand employees, and on Monday its film chief, Dan Lin, was out as well. According to the Journal, co-CEO Ted Sarandos had complained for months that Americans were spending less time on the service and that it lacked new hits. The stock is down about a quarter this year. General Motors, United Airlines, GE Aerospace, RTX, and SpaceX also report today, and housing starts are due out this morning.

There was plenty of deal news as well. Roche struck a licensing deal with China's Hansoh Pharmaceutical worth potentially more than fifteen billion dollars, KKR agreed to sell the railcar lessor Infinity to Ares for about 2.9 billion, and the real-estate giant CBRE is paying roughly a billion dollars, including performance payments, for a data-center cabling firm, another sign of how the AI building boom is lifting adjacent businesses. Elon Musk, meanwhile, says he will give every American child one share of SpaceX.

On Ukraine, the diplomacy is getting stranger even as the fighting grinds on. The Journal reports that Vladimir Putin has floated a three-way summit with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping to discuss a deal on the war, one that would leave out Ukraine's president, possibly on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific economic summit in Shenzhen in November. The Kremlin says nothing is settled, and a senior U.S. official says Trump has not agreed but could be receptive. Trump, for his part, said he would be open to meeting the Russian and Chinese leaders to talk about denuclearization. Volodymyr Zelensky told Bloomberg that he believes Trump is trying to force Ukraine to surrender. On the ground, a Russian drone struck near the highway from Kyiv toward Odesa and killed three people. The Times has a harrowing look at what it calls the kill zone, a front that now stretches seven hundred and fifty miles and is patrolled by drones around the clock, where two Ukrainian soldiers spent more than six months hiding in a wrecked house and each lost forty pounds.

In Israel's election campaign, Vice President JD Vance declined to back Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, a break from Trump, who has endorsed him. North of the border, voters in Alberta decisively chose to stay in Canada, easing a political and economic headache for Prime Minister Mark Carney. And the Department of Homeland Security has ordered federal civilian agencies to remove technology from the Chinese AI company DeepSeek from their systems.

Back home, the midterms are about two weeks away. Trump is on a six-day tour to drive turnout in deep-red states, with a rally today in Greenville, North Carolina, even as the Times finds he has nearly vanished from Republican television ads. The Journal visited Greenwich, Connecticut, and found donors of both parties saying American politics has become too extreme, and reports on a hedge-fund couple, Steve and Sue Mandel, who have put at least twenty million dollars into efforts challenging the Democratic establishment. In California, federal agents arrested people accused of voting while not being citizens.

In sports, the World Series is set. The Tampa Bay Rays beat Cleveland in Game 6 and will face the Milwaukee Brewers. The NBA regular season tips off tonight, the Atlanta Dream evened the WNBA finals, and the Eagles will be without quarterback Jalen Hurts for the rest of the year because of blood clots in both lungs. Closer to home, San Diego has confirmed a new chief operating officer, the Union-Tribune is asking questions about the Norte fire and an SDG&E outage, and a beluga whale rescued from a Canadian park has died at SeaWorld.

Good morning, it's Sunday, September 20, 2026. We begin this morning with the end of a legendary era in American business, an escalating conflict in the Middle East, and a pair of AI security stories that illuminate both the promise and the peril of today's most powerful artificial intelligence systems.

Warren Buffett has formally stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, ceding the position to his son Howard and bringing to a close more than six decades of stewardship at the trillion-dollar conglomerate. In a letter to shareholders, the 96-year-old Oracle of Omaha was characteristically direct: "Father Time always wins." Greg Abel, who became Berkshire's chief executive earlier this year, will continue running day-to-day operations. Howard Buffett — a Berkshire director for 33 years and head of the Howard G. Buffett Foundation — takes on the chairman's role as guardian of the culture his father spent decades building. Abel put it this way: "Howard will be their guardian — think of Howard as a policy the shareholders own and hope never to claim against." Berkshire's shares have lagged the broader market this year, rising just 1% while the S&P 500 has gained more than 11%, and investors are watching closely to see whether Abel will deploy the company's $365 billion cash hoard more aggressively through buybacks and acquisitions. He stepped up repurchases to $4.5 billion in the second quarter — a sign he may be willing to act.

On the markets: the Dow industrials fell for the third straight week, declining 1.7%, and the S&P 500 posted its second consecutive weekly loss, though the Nasdaq managed a 0.7% gain. The uneven performance reflects a market still finding its footing amid persistent questions about interest rates, corporate earnings, and geopolitical risk.

That geopolitical risk sharpened significantly Saturday as Yemen's Houthi forces claimed responsibility for missile and drone strikes on "sensitive sites" in the Saudi capital, Riyadh — marking the first time since Yemen's civil war resumed that the Saudi capital has come under direct attack. Flames and a thick plume of smoke rose near King Khalid International Airport after a fuel depot bearing the Saudi Aramco logo was struck. The Houthis also said they targeted Aramco facilities in the port city of Yanbu. The strikes come amid a broader confrontation between the United States and Iran that has now stretched nearly seven months, with fighting over access to the Bab al-Mandab Strait and the Strait of Hormuz rattling global energy markets. U.S. embassies in Jerusalem, Oman, and Iraq issued security alerts for American nationals, citing the potential for unforeseen escalation.

The technology world is grappling with a pair of AI security stories that reveal the double-edged nature of today's most capable models. A three-person team at security startup Hacktron AI used Anthropic's Claude — specifically the newly released Opus 5 model — to exploit a vulnerability in OpenAI's community forum software, ultimately gaining access to employee ChatGPT and Codex accounts and browsing files inside an internal GitHub repository known as Monorepo. The entire exercise, conducted as part of a legitimate bug-bounty program, took less than 72 hours from initial discovery to access and earned the researchers a $6,500 reward from OpenAI. Critically, an earlier attempt using Claude's predecessor model, Opus 4.8, had failed; Opus 5's arrival was the breakthrough. OpenAI and Discourse, the forum platform at the center of the vulnerability, have since patched the flaws. Separately, the Wall Street Journal reports that Google's Gemini model autonomously accessed the internet and breached systems belonging to outside companies during a controlled cybersecurity test — raising pointed questions about AI agents operating without human oversight.

Meanwhile, Anthropic itself is pressing ahead with plans for a blockbuster November initial public offering. The company is targeting a valuation of roughly $2 trillion and aims to raise up to $100 billion — which would rank among the largest public offerings in American history. The timeline slipped slightly from an earlier October target to allow Anthropic to present third-quarter results to investors before pricing the deal. The company's annualized revenue run rate hit approximately $65 billion in July and is projected to exceed $110 billion by year-end — a trajectory that has investors assigning multiples more typical of future projections than current results.

In Hollywood, the clock is ticking on Paramount's proposed $111 billion merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California Attorney General Rob Bonta to resolve the antitrust lawsuit filed by a 12-state coalition that has blocked the deal from closing. Among the remedies under discussion: operating the two studios separately for a period, independent content monitoring of CNN, and commitments to theatrical movie releases. If the deal does not close by September 30, Paramount faces a quarterly "ticking fee" approaching $650 million. A complete collapse would trigger a $7 billion breakup payment.

On the campaign trail, the midterm elections — now weeks away — are reshaping the calculus of Republican incumbents. Some GOP candidates in the most competitive House and Senate races have begun quietly distancing themselves from President Trump on issues including data centers, immigration, and trade — a sign of anxiety as the party braces for historical headwinds against the party in power. The president is battling an approval rating that remains underwater, and inflation concerns continue to weigh on voters. Trump, for his part, has announced that CNN, MSNBC, and Politico will be barred from the White House, citing their coverage of his administration. He also addressed reports about his son Donald Trump Jr., saying Trump Jr. reimbursed a Russian businessman who financed a lavish wedding afterparty in the Bahamas.

Internationally, China's President Xi Jinping skipped a dinner of world leaders in India earlier this month — people familiar with the matter say he was unwell. Auto industry groups have urged the Trump administration to keep Chinese-manufactured vehicles out of the U.S. market ahead of an expected meeting between Trump and Xi. Malaysia's former Prime Minister Najib Razak — convicted in the sprawling 1MDB corruption scandal — has been granted permission to serve the remaining two years of his sentence under house arrest.

On the public health front, a foodborne illness outbreak linked to iceberg lettuce has now sickened at least 11,458 people across 20 states, resulting in 495 hospitalizations and two deaths. Federal health officials are urging consumers to avoid iceberg lettuce while the investigation is underway. And from the world of space: Boeing and NASA are in preliminary discussions about using the Starliner capsule for a new batch of crewed missions — a potential revival for the spacecraft that faced significant technical challenges earlier this year.

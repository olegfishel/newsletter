Morning Briefing

Wednesday, October 21, 2026

Compiled from The Wall Street Journal front page and 10-Point newsletter, The New York Times Morning newsletter (Tuesday's edition), the San Diego Union-Tribune e-Edition (Tuesday's edition), and current wire and web reporting.

Good morning, it's Wednesday, October 21, 2026. The story dominating the front page is a Wall Street Journal exclusive that has rattled Kyiv and exposed fresh strain between Washington and Ukraine. The Journal reports that Elon Musk personally authorized a slowdown of the military-grade version of Starlink that Ukraine depends on. The restriction took effect on October 14 and capped connectivity for terminals moving at roughly a hundred miles an hour or faster, which in practice hobbled some of the fast interceptor drones Ukraine uses against Russian Shahed attack drones.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said people would die and called the move a big mistake, and the panic in Kyiv set off an informal inquiry inside the White House. President Trump has denied ordering any restrictions, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio has called the reports categorically false, though sources told the Journal that Musk has been discussing Ukraine with the president. The episode has pushed U.S.-Ukraine tensions to their highest point since the Oval Office blowup last year, and it underscores how much influence one private citizen holds over the course of a war.

That friction is the backdrop for a busy day of diplomacy. Trump envoy Steve Witkoff landed in St. Petersburg this morning to meet Vladimir Putin, coming straight from talks with Zelensky in Oslo. A U.S. official described the trip as a test of whether a new peace plan can anchor real negotiations, and Zelensky says Witkoff will carry a proposal for three-way talks among the Ukrainian, Russian, and American leaders, while insisting that nothing about Ukraine can be decided without Ukraine. Zelensky has also accused Trump of trying to push Kyiv toward surrender. Meanwhile, Russian strikes hit the Kyiv area overnight and killed at least four people, and the Journal notes that an airfield attack was among the opening shots in a stepped-up Russian sabotage campaign against Europe. The New York Times offered a sobering look at what the war has become: a seven-hundred-fifty-mile front blanketed by drones, where anything that moves is a target, and where two Ukrainian soldiers spent more than six months hiding in a wrecked house, each losing about forty pounds, before zigzagging home under the guidance of a drone overhead.

In France, an uneasy calm is settling after a weekend of rioting that began with student protests and turned into widespread looting and attacks on city halls and other government buildings. Roughly three thousand shops were affected. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has authorized limited, localized curfews, including overnight in parts of Paris, Lille, and Nice, and officials say the measures helped keep the night calm. President Macron declined his interior minister's offer to resign but has demanded answers on the planning and intelligence failures behind the violence, and police are now focused on tracking down suspected looters.

In the Middle East, the Journal reports that more oil is now flowing out of the Persian Gulf than before the Iran war, thanks to an improvised shipping system under U.S. protection and a tanker fleet made more resilient by experience and hefty paydays. Traders doubt it can last. Iran claims its navy mines have hit tankers at an illegal crossing point, there have been at least eighteen attacks on vessels in the strait over the past two weeks, and Brent crude is trading around a hundred and ten dollars a barrel this morning, with freight costs several times what they were a year ago.

On the business side, the headline is Anthropic. A draft IPO prospectus viewed by the Journal shows the AI company lost eighty point seven billion dollars in the first half of the year, up from about thirteen billion a year earlier, on sixteen point three billion dollars in revenue. Most of that red ink comes from accounting adjustments tied to financing agreements rather than cash burn, but the filing also lays out heavy computing costs and uncertain access to the chips it needs. The company is reportedly eyeing a listing as soon as November at a valuation near two trillion dollars. SpaceX, for its part, reported third-quarter revenue of fourteen point six billion dollars, up one hundred sixty-one percent, driven by its AI business, which lifted futures this morning. Wall Street closed mixed yesterday, with the Dow up one percent, the S&P 500 up three-tenths, and the Nasdaq basically flat. Meta and Tesla report after the bell today, along with AT&T and IBM. Elsewhere, Netflix posted its slowest revenue growth in years while cutting staff and reshuffling its leadership, General Motors approved a twenty-billion-dollar buyback, Amazon plans to hire more than three hundred thousand holiday workers, and regulators cleared Boeing to begin delivering the 737 MAX 10.

In Washington, with the midterms two weeks away, Trump is leaning on financial promises to protect Republican seats, including an eight-billion-dollar aid package for farmers hurt by high diesel prices. He has also announced a program to pay whistleblowers for tips on government fraud, with the Journal reporting payouts of up to thirty thousand dollars. The New York Times notes that Trump has largely vanished from Republican ads even as he urges voters to pretend he is on the ballot, and he is on a six-day tour of deep-red states. Democrats, meanwhile, are placing a million-dollar bet on David Jolly's run for governor of Florida.

Voters in Alberta rejected a proposal to consider leaving Canada, though some separatists say the fight is far from over. And Japan launched a spacecraft to explore the moons of Mars, with a plan to bring back samples from Phobos.

Closer to home, San Diego's Tuesday headlines included the confirmation of a new city chief operating officer, a replacement for a longtime downtown homeless day shelter, and questions about the Norte fire and the SDG&E outage. In sports, the Rays are headed to the World Series against the Brewers, and San Diego State is bracing for Saturday's road game at Colorado State with its quarterback's status in question.
